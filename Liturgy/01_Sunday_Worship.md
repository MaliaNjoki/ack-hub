# Sunday Worship in the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK)

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a structured overview of Sunday worship in the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK) for the ACK Hub knowledge base.

It describes the principal elements, participants, liturgical context and organisational considerations associated with Sunday worship without reproducing the copyrighted wording of authorised prayer books or service books.

---

## 2. ACK Liturgical Context

Sunday worship is a principal expression of the gathered life of the Church. Anglican worship combines Scripture, prayer, praise, preaching, sacramental life and congregational participation.

The ACK's principal modern liturgical resource is **Our Modern Services**, adopted by the Province of Kenya in 2002. ACK Uzima Publishing House has published the services in several languages, including English, Kiswahili, Kikuyu, Luo and Kamba, with some services also available in Kisii and Luyia.

The authorised current service books and the relevant diocesan or provincial directions remain the primary authority for the exact order, wording and rubrics of a particular service.

---

## 3. Principal Elements of Sunday Worship

A Sunday service may include the following broad elements:

1. **Gathering of the Congregation**

   * Procession where applicable
   * Opening greeting
   * Opening prayer
   * Hymns, songs or praise
   * Preparation for worship

2. **Confession and Absolution**

   * Corporate confession
   * Declaration of God's forgiveness/absolution by the authorised minister

3. **Liturgy of the Word**

   * Old Testament reading where appointed
   * Psalm
   * New Testament reading
   * Gospel reading
   * Sermon or exposition of Scripture
   * Creed or affirmation of faith where appointed
   * Prayers of intercession

4. **Response and Offering**

   * Congregational response to God's Word
   * Offering and thanksgiving
   * Additional prayers or acts of worship as authorised

5. **Holy Communion**

   * Where Holy Communion is celebrated, the service proceeds according to the authorised Eucharistic order.
   * The detailed Communion structure is documented separately in `Liturgy/02_Holy_Communion.md`.

6. **Notices and Congregational Matters**

   * Church announcements
   * Ministry information
   * Notices concerning activities and pastoral life

7. **Conclusion and Dismissal**

   * Post-service prayers where appointed
   * Blessing
   * Dismissal
   * Recessional or closing hymn/song where applicable

The exact sequence varies according to the authorised service being used, the Church calendar and local diocesan/parish practice.

---

## 4. Scripture and Preaching

Holy Scripture is central to Sunday worship.

The appointed readings provide the biblical foundation for the service, while preaching or teaching assists the congregation in understanding and applying God's Word.

The ACK Decade Strategy 2018–2027 identifies the development of a theologically sound and harmonised Sunday School curriculum and the development of relevant forms of worship as strategic areas within Christian formation and mission.

---

## 5. Prayer and Congregational Participation

Anglican worship involves active participation by the congregation.

Depending on the service, participation may include:

* Corporate prayers
* Confession
* Responses
* Scripture readings
* Singing hymns and worship songs
* Recitation of the Creed
* Intercessions
* Giving and thanksgiving
* Receiving Holy Communion where celebrated
* Responding to the dismissal

The exact responses and texts should always be taken from the authorised service book rather than reconstructed from unofficial sources.

---

## 6. Ministers and Other Participants

Sunday worship may involve several authorised ministries and service roles, depending on the local congregation and service.

These may include:

* Bishop
* Priest
* Deacon
* Lay ministers
* Readers
* Intercessors
* Worship and music teams
* Choir
* Servers
* Sunday School/Children's Ministry workers
* Ushers
* Other authorised congregational participants

The roles and permissions of particular ministers should be understood in accordance with ACK constitutional, canonical, liturgical and diocesan requirements.

---

## 7. Holy Communion on Sunday

Holy Communion may form part of Sunday worship according to the authorised service and local practice.

The Eucharistic service includes the central actions of thanksgiving, consecration, breaking of bread and administration of Holy Communion according to the authorised liturgical form.

For the structured overview of Holy Communion, see:

`Liturgy/02_Holy_Communion.md`

**Source-control rule:** Exact Eucharistic prayers, responses, rubrics and other prayer-book wording must be retrieved from an authorised ACK edition rather than reproduced from memory or unofficial websites.

## 8. Children and Sunday Worship

Children are part of the worshipping life and mission of the ACK.

The ACK identifies Sunday School as part of its mission structure. ACK historical material also documents children's ministry activities including Sunday School, children's conferences, teachers' conferences, music and drama festivals, and Boys' and Girls' Brigade activities.

Children's ministry should therefore be understood as part of the wider life of the Church rather than as a completely separate worshipping community.

See also:

`05_Policies_and_Departments/02_Provincial_Boards/Board_of_Mission/Childrens_Mission.md`

## 9. Languages

ACK worship may be conducted in English, Kiswahili and other Kenyan languages according to the congregation, diocese and available authorised liturgical resources.

ACK Uzima Publishing House records that *Our Modern Services* has been published in several languages used by the Anglican Church in Kenya.

Language use may therefore vary between dioceses and congregations while remaining within the authorised Anglican liturgical framework.

---

## 10. The Church Calendar

Sunday worship is shaped by the Church's liturgical calendar.

The character of worship may change according to:

* Advent
* Christmas
* Epiphany
* Lent
* Holy Week
* Easter
* Pentecost
* Trinity
* Ordinary/Trinity Season
* Saints' days and other appointed commemorations
* Special diocesan or provincial occasions

For liturgical seasons and colours, see:

`Liturgical_Seasons_and_Colours.md`

The current ACK Church Diary and Lectionary should take precedence when determining appointed readings, seasons, colours and special observances.

---

## 11. Special Sundays and Services

Some Sundays may include special observances or themes determined by:

* The provincial Church
* A diocese
* An archdeaconry
* A parish
* The Church calendar
* An authorised church programme or initiative

Examples may include mission-related Sundays, youth and children's activities, thanksgiving services, harvest celebrations and other authorised congregational observances.

Such occasions should not automatically be treated as universal ACK liturgical requirements unless supported by an authoritative ACK source.

---

## 12. Parish and Diocesan Variation

The broad Anglican structure of worship is shared across the Province, but the practical expression of Sunday worship may differ between congregations.

Variation may result from:

* Language
* Local culture
* Congregational context
* Available ministers
* Diocesan direction
* The particular authorised service being used
* Liturgical season
* Special church occasions

Local variation should not be confused with changes to the authorised doctrine or liturgical requirements of the Church.
