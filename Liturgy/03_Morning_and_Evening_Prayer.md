# Morning and Evening Prayer in the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK)

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a structured reference to Morning and Evening Prayer within the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

It describes the purpose, broad structure and liturgical context of daily prayer without reproducing the complete wording of authorised ACK prayer books or service materials.

The current authorised ACK liturgical resources remain the primary authority for the exact order, prayers, responses and rubrics.

---

## 2. Liturgical Context

Morning and Evening Prayer form part of the Anglican tradition of daily corporate and personal prayer.

They provide opportunities for the Church to:

* Praise God
* Hear Scripture
* Confess and seek forgiveness
* Pray for the Church and the world
* Give thanks
* Reflect on God's Word
* Commit the day to God
* End the day in prayer and thanksgiving

The form used by an ACK congregation should follow the authorised liturgical resources applicable to the Church and the particular occasion.

---

## 3. Morning Prayer

Morning Prayer provides a framework for beginning the day with God.

A service may include broad elements such as:

1. **Opening and Preparation**

   * Greeting
   * Opening sentences or introductory responses
   * Opening prayer

2. **Praise and Confession**

   * Praise
   * Confession where appointed
   * Declaration of forgiveness/absolution where authorised

3. **The Ministry of the Word**

   * Scripture readings
   * Psalm or psalms
   * Canticle or other biblical response where appointed
   * Reflection, sermon or homily where appropriate

4. **Affirmation of Faith**

   * Creed or other authorised affirmation where appointed

5. **Intercession**

   * Prayers for the Church
   * Prayers for the world
   * Prayers for communities and leaders
   * Prayers for those in need
   * Thanksgiving

6. **Conclusion**

   * Concluding prayer
   * Blessing where authorised
   * Dismissal

The exact order depends on the authorised form being used.

---

## 4. Evening Prayer

Evening Prayer provides an opportunity for the Church to gather at the close of the day.

A service may include:

1. **Opening and Preparation**

   * Greeting
   * Opening prayer or responses

2. **Confession and Reflection**

   * Examination of the day
   * Confession where appointed
   * Declaration of forgiveness/absolution where authorised

3. **The Ministry of the Word**

   * Scripture readings
   * Psalms
   * Canticles or other biblical responses where appointed
   * Reflection, sermon or homily where appropriate

4. **Affirmation of Faith**

   * Creed or other authorised affirmation where appointed

5. **Intercession and Thanksgiving**

   * Prayers for the Church and world
   * Prayers for those in need
   * Thanksgiving for the day
   * Prayers for protection and guidance

6. **Conclusion**

   * Concluding prayers
   * Blessing where authorised
   * Dismissal

---

## 5. Scripture and the Psalms

Scripture is central to Morning and Evening Prayer.

The services may incorporate:

* Psalms
* Old Testament readings
* New Testament readings
* Gospel readings
* Canticles
* Biblical responses
* Other appointed passages

The current ACK Church Diary and Lectionary should be used when determining appointed readings and psalms.

---

## 6. Prayer and Intercession

Intercession forms an important part of the daily prayer tradition.

Prayers may address:

* The Church
* The Archbishop and bishops
* Clergy and lay ministers
* The government and public authorities
* Communities
* Families
* Schools and institutions
* The sick and vulnerable
* People experiencing hardship
* Peace and justice
* The environment
* Mission and evangelism
* The wider world

Local intercessions may be added where permitted by the authorised service.

---

## 7. Morning and Evening Prayer in Congregational Life

Morning and Evening Prayer may be used:

* In parish worship
* In smaller congregational gatherings
* During clergy and ministry gatherings
* In retreats
* In conferences
* In church institutions
* In households and personal devotion

The particular setting does not remove the need to follow authorised liturgical requirements where a formal ACK service is being conducted.

## 8. Personal and Corporate Prayer

The Anglican tradition recognises both personal and corporate prayer.

### Personal Prayer

Individuals may use Morning and Evening Prayer as part of their daily devotional life.

This may include:

* Scripture reading
* Meditation
* Personal confession
* Intercession
* Thanksgiving
* Personal petitions

### Corporate Prayer

The Church may gather for Morning or Evening Prayer as a community.

Corporate prayer emphasises:

* Common worship
* Shared Scripture
* Collective intercession
* Christian fellowship
* Unity in prayer

Music should support the worship and prayer of the congregation and remain consistent with the authorised liturgical context.

Morning and Evening Prayer may involve:

## 11. Relationship to Holy Communion

Morning or Evening Prayer is distinct from Holy Communion.

Morning and Evening Prayer focus primarily on:

* Prayer
* Scripture
* Praise
* Confession
* Intercession
* Thanksgiving

Holy Communion additionally includes the Eucharistic celebration.

## 13. Language and Local Practice

ACK worship may take place in English, Kiswahili and other Kenyan languages according to the congregation and available authorised resources.

Local practice may influence:

* Language
* Music
* Congregational responses
* Duration
* Leadership arrangements
* Frequency of services

Such variations should not be presented as universal ACK requirements unless supported by an authoritative source.

---

## 14. Historical and Liturgical Development

The ACK has developed and adapted Anglican liturgical resources for the Kenyan context.

Modern ACK liturgical development includes the Modern Service of Holy Communion produced by the Provincial Liturgical Committee and subsequent development and indigenisation of modern services.

*Our Modern Services* was adopted by the Province of Kenya in 2002.

Historical forms should be distinguished from the current authorised practice.
