# Church Calendar and Special Services

## Purpose

This file separates information about the Church Year into two categories:

1. **ACK-specific facts** — information directly supported by Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK) sources.
2. **Anglican and wider Christian tradition** — broader liturgical information that provides context but should not automatically be treated as an ACK-specific rule.

This distinction is important for accurate ACK Hub retrieval.

---

# Part I — ACK-Specific Information

## 1. ACK and the Church Calendar

The Anglican Church of Kenya uses a **Church Diary and Lectionary** as part of its worship and church-life resources.

ACK's official material records the Church Diary and Lectionary as part of its publishing and liturgical-resource history. ACK Uzima Publishing House states that it has published the Church Lectionary and Diary and other liturgical materials for the Anglican Church of Kenya.

ACK's Decade Strategy also identifies information from the **Church Diary and Lectionary** as part of its description of the Church's organisation and history.

### ACK Hub rule

For a date-specific question, such as:

* "What are today's readings?"
* "What is this Sunday's liturgical season?"
* "What colour is prescribed today?"
* "What festival is observed today?"

the system should prefer the **current ACK Church Diary and Lectionary** rather than relying only on general Anglican tradition.

---

## 2. ACK Liturgical Publishing

ACK has an established institutional system for producing and distributing liturgical material.

ACK Uzima Publishing House identifies its objectives as including the production of liturgical materials for the Anglican Church of Kenya. Its catalogue has included:

* Modern English services.
* *Our Modern Services*.
* Kiswahili versions of modern services.
* Prayer books.
* Confirmation material.
* Hymn and other church literature.
* Church Lectionary and Diary material.

This establishes that ACK's worship resources should be treated as a distinct documentary tradition rather than assuming that every Anglican service-book practice is identical across provinces.

## 3. ACK Liturgical and Worship Resources

For ACK Hub, these official ACK resources should take priority when determining current ACK practice.

## 9. ACK Diocesan Variation

**Provincial practice**

from

**Diocesan practice**

**Parish/local practice**.

A diocesan custom should not automatically be presented as a province-wide ACK requirement.

# Part II — Anglican and Wider Christian Tradition

The following material provides **background context**. It should not automatically be labelled as an ACK-specific rule unless confirmed by an ACK source.

## 10. The Christian Year

The wider Anglican tradition organises worship around the Christian Year.

Common seasons and observances include:

* Advent.
* Christmas.
* Epiphany.
* Lent.
* Holy Week.
* Easter.
* Ascension.
* Pentecost.
* Trinity.
* The period following Pentecost.

These categories are useful for understanding Anglican worship, but their exact observance can differ between Anglican provinces and churches.

---

## 11. Advent — Anglican Tradition

Advent is traditionally associated with:

* Preparation.
* Expectation.
* Hope.
* Repentance.
* The coming of Christ.

Purple or violet is commonly associated with Advent in many Anglican traditions.

**ACK status:** Do not treat the colour or exact observance as an ACK rule without checking the current ACK calendar or authorised material.

---

## 12. Christmas — Anglican Tradition

Christmas celebrates the birth or Nativity of Jesus Christ.

Anglican churches may hold:

* Christmas Eve services.
* Christmas Day worship.
* Holy Communion.
* Carol services.
* Children's services.
* Family worship.

White or gold is traditionally associated with Christmas in many Anglican traditions.

**ACK status:** General Anglican context; exact ACK practice requires ACK-source verification.

---

## 13. Epiphany — Anglican Tradition

Epiphany traditionally concerns the manifestation or revelation of Christ.

Themes may include:

* Christ revealed to the nations.
* Mission.
* Light.
* Christian witness.

White or gold is commonly associated with Epiphany.

**ACK status:** General Anglican tradition unless confirmed by the current ACK calendar.

---

## 14. Lent — Anglican Tradition

Lent is traditionally a period of preparation before Easter.

Common themes include:

* Repentance.
* Prayer.
* Fasting.
* Self-examination.
* Discipleship.

Purple or violet is traditionally associated with Lent in many Anglican traditions.

**ACK status:** General Anglican tradition. Exact ACK observance should be verified from ACK sources.

---

## 15. Holy Week — Anglican Tradition

Holy Week traditionally recalls the final events of Christ's earthly ministry.

Common observances include:

* Palm Sunday.
* Maundy Thursday.
* Good Friday.
* Holy Saturday.
* Easter Vigil.

Different Anglican churches may observe these occasions differently.

**ACK status:** Do not assume that every listed ceremony or schedule is universally prescribed by ACK.

---

## 16. Palm Sunday — Anglican Tradition

Palm Sunday commemorates Christ's entry into Jerusalem.

Some Anglican churches use:

* Palm branches.
* Processions.
* Passion readings.
* Special preaching.
* Holy Communion.

**ACK status:** These are general Anglican practices unless confirmed in an ACK-authorised service.

---

## 17. Maundy Thursday — Anglican Tradition

Maundy Thursday traditionally recalls:

* The Last Supper.
* The Eucharist.
* Christ's command to love and serve.
* The events preceding the crucifixion.

Some churches incorporate foot washing.

**ACK status:** Foot washing or any particular ceremony should not be presented as universally required by ACK without an ACK source.

---

## 18. Good Friday — Anglican Tradition

Good Friday commemorates Christ's crucifixion and death.

Common Anglican observances may include:

* Passion readings.
* Sermons or meditations.
* Hymns.
* Prayer.
* Silence.
* Veneration or other local practices.

Liturgical colours can vary by tradition.

**ACK status:** Do not state a single colour or ceremony as the current ACK rule without checking the ACK calendar.

---

## 19. Holy Saturday and Easter Vigil — Anglican Tradition

Some Anglican churches observe an Easter Vigil.

A Vigil may include:

* Scripture readings.
* Prayer.
* Reflection on salvation history.
* Renewal of baptismal identity.
* Proclamation of the resurrection.

**ACK status:** Whether and how an Easter Vigil is observed should be verified against the applicable ACK or diocesan service material.

## 20. Easter — Anglican Tradition

Easter celebrates the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

Common Anglican practices include:

* Holy Communion.
* Special preaching.
* Music and singing.
* Processions.
* Baptismal observances.
* Family and children's worship.

White or gold is traditionally associated with Easter.

**ACK status:** General Anglican context unless confirmed by the current ACK calendar or service book.

## 21. Ascension — Anglican Tradition

Ascension traditionally commemorates Christ's ascension.

Themes commonly include:

* Christ's exaltation.
* Mission.
* Christian witness.
* Hope.

The traditional observance occurs forty days after Easter.

**ACK status:** The theological observance belongs to the wider Christian calendar; current ACK scheduling should be checked against the ACK Church Diary and Lectionary.

Pentecost traditionally commemorates the coming of the Holy Spirit upon the Church.

Common themes include:

* The Holy Spirit.
* Mission.
* Spiritual gifts.
* Christian witness.

Red is traditionally associated with Pentecost.

**ACK status:** General Anglican tradition unless confirmed through current ACK calendar documentation.

## 23. Trinity Sunday — Anglican Tradition

Trinity Sunday focuses on Christian teaching concerning the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

White is traditionally associated with Trinity Sunday in many Anglican traditions.

**ACK status:** General Anglican context; current ACK observance requires ACK-source verification.

The period following Pentecost is commonly associated with:

Green is traditionally associated with much of this period.

# Part IV — Data Classification Rules

## 29. Three-Level Evidence Model

Every calendar claim in ACK Hub should be classified as one of the following:

### Level A — ACK-specific and verified

Directly supported by:

* Current ACK Church Diary.
* Current ACK Lectionary.
* Authorised ACK service book.
* Official ACK publication.
* Official provincial or diocesan documentation.

### Level B — Anglican tradition

Supported by wider Anglican liturgical tradition but not independently verified as an ACK-specific rule.

### Level C — Local practice

A practice documented for:

* A particular diocese.
* Parish.
* Cathedral.
* Ministry.
* Congregation.

It should not be generalised to the whole ACK.

---

## 30. Source Priority

For an ACK-specific question, use this order:

1. Current authorised ACK Church Diary and Lectionary.
2. Current authorised ACK liturgical/service materials.
3. ACK Provincial Synod and official provincial documents.
4. Official ACK publications.
5. Official diocesan documents.
6. Wider Anglican sources.
7. General Christian/liturgical reference works.

A general Anglican source should not override a specific current ACK source.

---

## 31. Current Versus Historical Information

Calendar information is date-sensitive.

The system should distinguish:

* Current calendar.
* Historical calendar.
* General Anglican tradition.
* Local diocesan practice.

For example, an historical ACK calendar should not be used to answer what the current ACK calendar prescribes.
