# Commissioning and Ministry Services

## Purpose

This file provides a structured reference for commissioning and ministry-related services within the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

It describes the general purpose, participants, structure, and relationship of commissioning services to other forms of authorised ministry. It does **not** reproduce the wording of any authorised liturgical service.

---

## 1. Commissioning in the ACK

Commissioning is a formal act by which a person, group, or ministry team is publicly entrusted and prayed for in relation to a particular ministry, responsibility, mission, or period of service.

Commissioning should be distinguished from:

* **Ordination**, through which a person is admitted to an ordained order of ministry.
* **Appointment**, which concerns the formal assignment of a person to an office, position, or responsibility.
* **Institution or induction**, where applicable, which may formally install a person into a particular ecclesiastical office or ministry.
* **Licensing or authorisation**, where applicable, which may give a person permission to exercise a defined ministry.

The exact terminology and procedure may vary according to the ministry concerned and the authority under which the service is conducted.

---

## 2. ACK Ministry Context

The ACK recognises both ordained and lay participation in the ministry of the Church.

The ACK Decade Strategy describes the clergy as having specialised responsibilities in worship and rites, while noting that lay people undertake a substantial part of ACK ministry through voluntary service. The strategy also identifies the enhancement of lay ministry through formal and informal training.

Commissioning therefore may be associated with ministries in which people are formally recognised, entrusted, equipped, or sent for service.

---

## 3. Ministries That May Involve Commissioning

Depending on the authorised ministry and the relevant provincial, diocesan, or parish arrangements, commissioning may be associated with:

* Lay ministry
* Lay readers
* Catechetical ministry
* Evangelistic and mission work
* Youth ministry
* Children's ministry
* Sunday School ministry
* Ministry teams
* Church officers and ministry leaders
* Mission workers
* Chaplaincy-related ministry
* Community and outreach initiatives
* Special ministry assignments
* Ministry projects or teams established for a particular purpose

**Important:** this list should not be interpreted as meaning that every ministry has one universally prescribed provincial commissioning rite. The form and authority of commissioning depend on the relevant ACK structures and authorised service materials.

---

## 4. Preparation for Commissioning

Preparation may include:

1. Identification or nomination of the person or ministry team.
2. Confirmation of the ministry responsibility.
3. Appropriate training or orientation.
4. Pastoral and spiritual preparation.
5. Confirmation of the authority responsible for the ministry.
6. Preparation of the congregation or ministry community where appropriate.
7. Arrangements for the commissioning service.

Where the ministry requires particular training, licensing, or diocesan approval, those requirements should be completed before commissioning.

---

## 5. General Structure of a Commissioning Service

A commissioning service may broadly include:

### 5.1 Gathering

The congregation gathers for worship and the persons or group being commissioned are presented.

### 5.2 Presentation

The individuals or ministry team may be formally introduced to the minister and congregation.

The presentation identifies the ministry or responsibility for which they are being commissioned.

### 5.3 Introduction and Explanation

The minister may explain:

* The purpose of the ministry.
* Its place within the Church.
* The responsibilities of those being commissioned.
* The importance of faithful and accountable service.

### 5.4 Scripture and Teaching

Scripture readings, preaching, exhortation, or teaching may provide the theological foundation for the ministry.

The emphasis may include:

* Christian discipleship.
* Service.
* Stewardship.
* Mission.
* Faithfulness.
* Responsibility.
* Service to the Church and wider community.

### 5.5 Questions or Declarations

Those being commissioned may be asked questions concerning their willingness to undertake the ministry.

Depending on the service, these may address:

* Commitment to Christ.
* Faithfulness to the Church.
* Responsibilities of the ministry.
* Cooperation with church leadership.
* Conduct and accountability.
* Willingness to serve others.

### 5.6 Prayer

The minister and congregation pray for the individuals or group.

Prayer may seek:

* Wisdom.
* Strength.
* Faithfulness.
* Spiritual maturity.
* Protection.
* Guidance.
* Effectiveness in ministry.
* Unity within the Church.

### 5.7 Laying on of Hands

Where authorised or appropriate to the particular service, the commissioning may include the laying on of hands.

The use of this action should not automatically be interpreted as ordination. Its meaning depends upon the authorised service and the ministry being commissioned.

### 5.8 Commissioning and Declaration

The minister formally commissions or entrusts the individuals or team to their ministry.

The exact declaration should be taken from the authorised liturgical or service material applicable to the ministry.

### 5.9 Charge and Presentation

Those commissioned may receive a charge concerning faithful service.

The congregation may also be encouraged to support the ministry through:

* Prayer.
* Participation.
* Encouragement.
* Accountability.
* Practical support.

### 5.10 Blessing and Dismissal

The service concludes with prayer, blessing, and dismissal for ministry.

## 6. Commissioning and Lay Ministry

Lay ministry is an important part of ACK life.

The ACK Decade Strategy identifies an extensive network of lay organisations through which ministries and functions are conducted and states that the role of the laity is to be enhanced through training.

Commissioning can therefore provide a visible connection between:

* Training and service.
* Christian vocation and responsibility.
* Congregational support and ministry.
* Individual gifts and the mission of the Church.

---

## 7. Youth and Children's Ministry

Commissioning may also be associated with ministry involving young people and children.

ACK structures include youth and children's ministries, with the Board of Mission identifying KAYO and Children's Mission among its departments.

Where a particular youth, children's, Sunday School, or ministry-worker commissioning service is used, the exact rite should be verified against the authorised service material or the relevant diocesan authority.

---

## 8. Mission and Evangelism

Commissioning may be used when people are formally entrusted with mission, evangelism, outreach, or other forms of Christian service.

This is consistent with the ACK Board of Mission's responsibility for mission, evangelism, discipleship, preaching, and the spiritual growth of Christians.

Mission-related commissioning may therefore mark the beginning or formal recognition of a particular ministry assignment.

---

## 9. Chaplaincy and Special Ministries

Specialised ministries may require additional authorisation, training, or institutional arrangements.

Examples may include:

* Hospital chaplaincy.
* Prison chaplaincy.
* Military chaplaincy.
* Institutional chaplaincy.
* Mission assignments.
* Special outreach ministries.

ACK's provincial structures include chaplaincy within its ministry and education-related work.

The presence of a commissioning service does not replace any separate professional, institutional, diocesan, or legal requirements that may apply to a particular ministry.

---

## 10. Ministers and Participants

Participants may include:

* Archbishop.
* Bishop.
* Archdeacon.
* Priest or other authorised minister.
* Lay leaders.
* Persons being commissioned.
* Ministry team members.
* Sponsors or ministry representatives.
* Congregation.
* Family members and invited guests.

The appropriate presiding minister depends on the ministry and the authority under which the commissioning takes place.

---

## 11. Role of the Congregation

The congregation may participate through:

* Prayer.
* Responses.
* Affirmation of support.
* Witnessing the commissioning.
* Commitment to support the ministry.
* Continued prayer and encouragement.

Commissioning is therefore both a public recognition of ministry and an opportunity for the Church community to affirm its shared responsibility for mission.

---

## 12. Commissioning and Ordination

Commissioning must not be treated as interchangeable with ordination.

### Ordination

Ordination relates to the recognised orders of ordained ministry within the Church, including:

* Deacon.
* Priest.
* Bishop.

See [`08_Ordination.md`](08_Ordination.md).

### Commissioning

Commissioning normally concerns the entrusting or recognition of a particular ministry, responsibility, assignment, or service.

A person may therefore be commissioned for ministry without being ordained to Holy Orders.

---

## 13. Commissioning and Appointment

Appointment and commissioning may occur together, but they describe different aspects of ministry.

**Appointment** concerns the assignment of responsibility or office.

**Commissioning** publicly entrusts the person or group to undertake ministry and may provide prayer, recognition, and congregational support.

The exact relationship depends on the office or ministry involved.

---

## 14. Records and Documentation

Where a commissioning is formally conducted, appropriate records may be maintained by the relevant parish, archdeaconry, or diocese.

Depending on the ministry, records may include:

* Name of person or ministry team.
* Ministry or responsibility.
* Date of commissioning.
* Place of commissioning.
* Presiding minister.
* Relevant authority.
* Certificate or other documentation where issued.

The existence and retention requirements of such records should be verified against the applicable diocesan or provincial procedures.

---

## 15. Relationship to the Church Calendar

Commissioning services may be incorporated into:

* Sunday worship.
* Special ministry Sundays.
* Mission events.
* Youth or children's ministry events.
* Diocesan or provincial gatherings.
* Beginning of a ministry programme.
* Special outreach or mission initiatives.

The timing depends on the ministry and local church arrangements.

---

## 16. Parish and Diocesan Variation

Commissioning practices can differ between dioceses and ministries.

Variation may concern:

* Who presides.
* Who may be commissioned.
* Preparation requirements.
* Training requirements.
* Whether laying on of hands is included.
* Required documentation.
* Whether a particular authorised form is used.
* Relationship between commissioning and appointment.

The ACK Hub should therefore avoid presenting a locally used commissioning practice as a universal ACK rule unless an authoritative provincial source confirms it.

---

## 17. Authoritative Source Hierarchy

For commissioning and ministry services, use the following source order:

1. **Authorised ACK liturgical/service books and official provincial documents**
2. **ACK Constitution and other governing documents**
3. **Official ACK provincial publications**
4. **Official diocesan documents and authorised service materials**
5. **Official ACK announcements and reports**
6. **Established Anglican liturgical references**
7. **Secondary sources**, only where authoritative ACK material is unavailable

Where sources conflict, the current authorised ACK source should take precedence.
