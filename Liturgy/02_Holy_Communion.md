# Holy Communion in the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK)

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a structured reference to Holy Communion in the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

It describes the broad order, purpose, participants and liturgical context of Holy Communion without reproducing the complete wording of the authorised ACK prayer book.

The exact authorised service, prayers, responses and rubrics should always be obtained from the current ACK liturgical resources.

---

## 2. ACK Liturgical Context

Holy Communion is a central sacramental service within Anglican worship.

The ACK's modern liturgical tradition includes **Our Modern Services**, adopted by the Province of Kenya in 2002. The authorised service books provide the actual texts, responses and directions for the celebration of Holy Communion.

This document is therefore a knowledge-base summary and **not a replacement for the authorised liturgical book**.

---

## 3. Broad Structure of Holy Communion

The service can be understood through four broad movements:

1. **The Gathering of God's People**
2. **The Liturgy of the Word**
3. **The Liturgy of the Sacrament**
4. **The Dismissal**

The precise order may depend on the authorised form of service being used.

---

## 4. The Gathering of God's People

The congregation is gathered for worship and prepared to hear God's Word and participate in the sacramental celebration.

Elements may include:

* Processional hymn or song
* Greeting
* Opening prayer
* Prayer of preparation
* The Commandments/Decalogue where appointed
* Confession
* Absolution

The authorised service book determines the exact sequence and wording.

---

## 5. The Liturgy of the Word

The congregation hears and responds to Scripture.

Depending on the appointed service and Church calendar, this may include:

* Old Testament reading
* Psalm
* New Testament reading
* Gospel reading
* Gospel acclamation where appointed
* Sermon or exposition
* Creed or affirmation of faith
* Intercessions

The current ACK Church Diary and Lectionary should be used when determining appointed readings.

---

## 6. The Liturgy of the Sacrament

The congregation moves from hearing God's Word to the celebration of Holy Communion.

The broad elements may include:

### The Peace

The congregation participates in the exchange of peace according to the authorised form.

### Offertory

Offerings and gifts are presented as part of the congregation's response and thanksgiving.

### Eucharistic Prayer

The celebrant leads the Eucharistic prayer according to the authorised liturgical form.

This includes thanksgiving and the Church's prayer surrounding the sacramental celebration.

### The Words of Institution

The institution of Holy Communion is proclaimed using the authorised biblical and liturgical form.

### The Lord's Prayer

The congregation participates in the Lord's Prayer according to the authorised service.

### Breaking of Bread

The bread is broken as part of the Eucharistic action.

### Administration of Holy Communion

The consecrated bread and wine are administered to communicants according to the authorised practice of the Church.

**Important:** Exact Eucharistic prayers, responses, rubrics and ministerial directions must be taken from an authorised ACK service book and should not be reconstructed from memory.

---

## 7. The Dismissal

Following Holy Communion, the congregation participates in the concluding prayers and is sent out into the world.

The conclusion may include:

* Post-Communion prayer
* Blessing
* Dismissal
* Closing or recessional hymn/song

The exact form depends on the authorised service.

---

## 8. Ministers and Participants

Holy Communion may involve:

* Bishop
* Priest
* Deacon
* Licensed or authorised lay ministers where applicable
* Servers
* Readers
* Intercessors
* Choir or worship team
* Ushers
* Congregation

The responsibilities of ministers and other participants should be determined by the ACK Constitution, authorised liturgical resources and applicable diocesan requirements.

## 9. The Eucharistic Elements

Holy Communion involves the sacramental use of:

* Bread
* Wine

The administration and handling of the sacramental elements must follow the authorised practice of the Church.

The ACK Hub should not infer detailed sacramental procedures where the authoritative liturgical source has not been verified.

## 10. Communion and Congregational Participation

Holy Communion is a corporate act of Christian worship.

Congregational participation may include:

* Listening to Scripture
* Corporate confession
* Prayer
* Responses
* Affirmation of faith
* Sharing the Peace
* Offering
* Participation in the Eucharistic celebration
* Receiving Holy Communion where appropriate
* Thanksgiving
* Responding to the dismissal

The exact eligibility, preparation and pastoral procedures for receiving Communion should be taken from current authorised ACK guidance rather than assumed.

Holy Communion may form part of Sunday worship.

When it does, the service moves from the gathering and proclamation of God's Word into the Eucharistic celebration before concluding with thanksgiving and dismissal.

## 13. Historical Development in the ACK

The ACK has developed and adapted its liturgical resources over time.

The Provincial Liturgical Committee produced a Modern Service of Holy Communion in 1989, followed by further development and indigenisation of modern services.

**Our Modern Services** was subsequently adopted by the Province of Kenya in 2002.

Historical developments should not automatically be treated as the current authorised liturgical practice.
