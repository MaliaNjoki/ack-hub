# Funeral and Burial in the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK)

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a structured reference to funeral and burial ministry in the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

It covers the broad pastoral, liturgical and congregational context of funeral and burial services without reproducing complete copyrighted funeral liturgy.

The current authorised ACK liturgical resources, Church regulations and applicable diocesan guidance remain the primary authorities for exact service orders, prayers and procedures.

---

## 2. Funeral Ministry in the ACK

Funeral ministry provides pastoral care to people following death and offers an opportunity for the Church to gather in prayer, proclaim Christian hope and commend the deceased to God.

Funeral ministry may involve:

* Pastoral care
* Prayer
* Scripture
* Hymns and worship
* Thanksgiving and remembrance
* Proclamation of the Christian faith
* Comfort for mourners
* Commendation of the deceased
* Burial or other authorised committal
* Continuing pastoral support

The exact service depends on the circumstances and the authorised liturgical form being used.

---

## 3. Pastoral Care of the Bereaved

The Church's ministry extends beyond the funeral service itself.

Pastoral care may include:

* Visiting the bereaved
* Prayer
* Counselling and spiritual support
* Assistance with funeral arrangements
* Congregational support
* Support for children and vulnerable family members
* Follow-up after burial
* Referral to appropriate professional support where necessary

Clergy and pastoral workers should observe applicable safeguarding, confidentiality and pastoral-care requirements.

---

## 4. Preparation for a Funeral

Funeral preparation may involve the family, clergy, parish leadership and other relevant participants.

Practical matters may include:

* Meeting with the family
* Establishing the circumstances of death
* Confirming the date and place of service
* Preparing Scripture readings
* Selecting hymns or songs
* Preparing a sermon or homily
* Arranging participation by family and congregation
* Coordinating burial arrangements
* Completing required Church records
* Liaising with relevant civil authorities where necessary

Exact procedures may vary by diocese and parish.

---

## 5. Broad Structure of a Funeral Service

The exact order depends on the authorised ACK service.

A funeral service may broadly include:

### A. Gathering

The congregation gathers to support the bereaved and worship God.

### B. Opening Prayers

The service begins with authorised prayers and introductory elements.

### C. Scripture

Readings from Holy Scripture provide the biblical foundation for the service.

### D. Hymns and Worship

The congregation may participate through hymns, songs and other authorised forms of worship.

### E. Sermon or Homily

The minister proclaims and explains the Christian message in the context of death, grief and hope.

### F. Thanksgiving and Remembrance

The service may provide an opportunity to give thanks for the life of the deceased and acknowledge the significance of the person's life and relationships.

### G. Prayers

Prayers may be offered for:

* The deceased
* The bereaved family
* Friends and community
* The Church
* Those experiencing grief
* God's comfort and strength

### H. Commendation

The deceased may be commended to God according to the authorised liturgical form.

### I. Committal and Burial

The body is committed according to the authorised burial or committal service.

### J. Conclusion

The service concludes with prayer, blessing and dismissal according to the authorised form.

---

## 6. Burial and Committal

Burial is distinct from the wider funeral service.

A committal or burial service may take place at:

* A cemetery
* A family burial place
* Another location permitted by applicable law and Church requirements

The exact prayers, actions and procedures must follow the authorised ACK service.

The ACK Hub should not infer detailed burial procedures from general Anglican practice where an ACK-specific source is required.

---

## 7. Cremation and Other Circumstances

Where a funeral involves circumstances other than a conventional burial, the appropriate authorised ACK and diocesan guidance should be consulted.

The ACK Hub should not assume that every funeral circumstance follows the same procedure.

Questions involving:

* Cremation
* Burial outside the usual parish
* Death abroad
* Repatriation
* Unidentified remains
* Multiple funerals
* Special memorial services

should be verified against current Church and applicable legal requirements.

---

## 8. Ministers and Participants

A funeral service may involve:

* Bishop
* Priest
* Deacon where authorised
* Family members
* Readers
* Choir or worship team
* Servers
* Church leaders
* Congregation
* Other authorised participants

The minister conducting the service should follow the applicable ACK liturgical and ecclesiastical requirements.

---

## 9. Congregational Participation

Funeral worship is a communal expression of Christian care.

The congregation may participate through:

* Prayer
* Scripture readings
* Hymns and songs
* Responses
* Listening to the sermon
* Thanksgiving
* Supporting the bereaved
* Participating in the committal where appropriate

The Church community has an important pastoral role before, during and after the funeral.

---

## 10. Children and Young People

Children and young people may experience grief differently from adults.

Pastoral ministry should therefore consider:

* Age-appropriate communication
* Emotional and spiritual support
* Participation in the funeral where appropriate
* Safeguarding
* Family support
* Referral to suitable professional assistance where necessary

The ACK Hub should not provide medical or psychological diagnosis but may identify the need for appropriate professional support.

---

## 11. Scripture and Christian Hope

Holy Scripture is central to Christian funeral worship.

Scriptural readings and preaching may address themes such as:

* God's presence
* Hope
* Resurrection
* Eternal life
* Faith
* Comfort
* Love
* Christian community

The specific readings should be taken from the authorised ACK Church Diary and Lectionary or applicable funeral service.

---

## 12. Funeral Records and Archives

Funeral and burial records are important Church records.

The ACK Provincial Archives identifies burial records and related Church activities among records preserved by the Church.

Parishes should maintain appropriate records according to current ACK administrative and archival requirements.

Records may include:

* Name of deceased
* Date of death
* Date of funeral
* Date of burial
* Place of burial
* Parish
* Minister
* Relevant register information

The exact record fields and retention requirements should be verified against current ACK archival and administrative guidance.

---

## 13. Memorial and Thanksgiving Services

The Church may also provide pastoral services associated with remembrance.

These may include:

* Memorial services
* Thanksgiving services
* Anniversary prayers
* Family prayers
* Other authorised pastoral gatherings

Such services should not automatically be treated as identical to the formal funeral or burial service.

Funeral services may take place throughout the Church year, subject to applicable Church and local arrangements.

The liturgical season may influence:

* Music

## 15. Parish and Diocesan Variation

Funeral arrangements may differ between ACK dioceses and parishes.

Variation may include:

* Preparation procedures
* Funeral scheduling
* Burial arrangements
* Documentation
* Music
* Language
* Family participation
* Memorial practices
* Pastoral follow-up

## 16. Authoritative Sources

For ACK funeral and burial information, use this source hierarchy:

1. **Current authorised ACK funeral and burial service**
2. **Current ACK Constitution and Church regulations**
3. **Current ACK Church Diary and Lectionary**
4. **Official ACK provincial directions**
5. **Official diocesan or parish guidance**
