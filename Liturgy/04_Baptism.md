# Baptism in the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK)

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a structured reference to Baptism in the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

It describes the theological and liturgical context, participants, preparation and broad structure of baptismal services without reproducing the complete wording of authorised ACK service books.

The current authorised ACK liturgical resources remain the primary authority for the exact order, prayers, declarations, responses and rubrics.

---

## 2. Baptism in the ACK

Baptism is a Christian sacramental rite through which a person is incorporated into the life of the Church.

Within the Anglican tradition, Baptism is associated with:

* Christian initiation
* Entry into the visible life of the Church
* Repentance and faith
* God's grace
* New life in Christ
* Membership in the Christian community
* Commitment to Christian discipleship

The authorised ACK liturgical service should be used when conducting Baptism.

---

## 3. Candidates for Baptism

Baptism may involve:

* Infants and young children
* Older children
* Young people
* Adults

The preparation and pastoral process may differ according to the candidate's age and circumstances.

Where infants or children are baptised, parents and sponsors/godparents have responsibilities associated with supporting the child's Christian formation.

Adult candidates may be expected to receive appropriate Christian instruction and demonstrate their understanding and commitment according to the authorised requirements of the Church.

---

## 4. Preparation for Baptism

Baptism should be approached as both a liturgical and pastoral responsibility.

Preparation may include:

* Christian instruction
* Understanding the meaning of Baptism
* Preparation of parents and sponsors
* Preparation of adult candidates
* Understanding Christian responsibilities
* Prayer and spiritual preparation
* Administrative registration
* Confirmation of eligibility according to applicable Church requirements

The exact preparation requirements may be determined by the diocese, parish and authorised Church regulations.

---

## 5. Parents and Sponsors

For the Baptism of children, parents and sponsors/godparents have an important role in supporting the child's Christian life.

Their responsibilities may include:

* Supporting Christian instruction
* Encouraging participation in worship
* Supporting prayer and Bible reading
* Encouraging Christian character and discipleship
* Supporting the child's participation in the life of the Church

The precise declarations and commitments made by parents and sponsors must be taken from the authorised baptismal service.

---

## 6. Broad Structure of Baptism

The exact order depends on the authorised ACK service.

A baptismal service may broadly include:

### A. Presentation

The candidate or candidates are presented to the Church.

### B. Questions and Declarations

The minister addresses the candidates, parents or sponsors as applicable.

These may concern:

* Faith
* Repentance
* Renunciation of evil
* Commitment to Christian discipleship
* Responsibilities of parents and sponsors

### C. Profession of Faith

The candidate, parents or sponsors may participate in an authorised profession or affirmation of Christian faith.

### D. Prayer and Thanksgiving

The Church gives thanks and prays for the candidates and for the work of God in their lives.

### E. Baptism with Water

The candidate is baptised with water according to the authorised form.

### F. Sign and Declaration

The service may include authorised signs, declarations and prayers associated with incorporation into the Christian community.

### G. Welcome into the Church

The newly baptised person is welcomed into the life and fellowship of the Church.

### H. Concluding Prayers and Blessing

The service concludes according to the authorised liturgical form.

---

## 7. Water and the Sacramental Sign

Water is the principal physical element used in Baptism.

It serves as the visible sacramental sign associated with Baptism.

The ACK Hub should not infer detailed rules concerning the amount, method or administration of baptismal water unless these are supported by an authorised ACK source.

## 8. Baptism and Christian Formation

Baptism is connected to continuing Christian formation.

Following Baptism, the Church has a responsibility to support the person's growth in:

* Scripture
* Prayer
* Worship
* Christian fellowship
* Service
* Discipleship
* Christian character

For children, this formation may involve Sunday School and other children's ministry structures.

## 9. Baptism and Confirmation

Baptism and Confirmation are related but distinct aspects of Christian initiation and formation.

Baptism marks entry into the Christian life and Church.

Confirmation is treated separately within the ACK liturgical framework and provides an opportunity for appropriate candidates to affirm and mature in the Christian faith.

See:

`Liturgy/05_Confirmation.md`

for the dedicated Confirmation reference.

The exact relationship and pastoral requirements should be interpreted according to current authorised ACK guidance.

---

## 10. Baptismal Records

Baptisms are important Church records.

The ACK Provincial Archives identifies records relating to Baptism among the historical records preserved by the Church.

Parishes should maintain appropriate baptismal records according to applicable ACK administrative and archival requirements.

Relevant information may include:

* Candidate's name
* Date of birth where applicable
* Date of Baptism
* Place of Baptism
* Parents' names where applicable
* Sponsors/godparents where applicable
* Minister
* Relevant registration or record information

The exact fields and retention requirements should be verified against current ACK administrative and archival guidance.

---

## 11. Ministers and Participants

A baptismal service may involve:

* Bishop
* Priest
* Deacon where authorised
* Baptismal candidates
* Parents
* Sponsors/godparents
* Congregation
* Servers or other authorised assistants

The minister responsible for the service should follow the applicable ACK liturgical and ecclesiastical requirements.

---

## 12. Congregational Participation

Baptism is not solely a private ceremony.

The congregation may participate through:

* Prayer
* Responses
* Profession of faith
* Witnessing the baptism
* Welcoming the newly baptised person
* Supporting Christian formation

The Church community therefore has an ongoing role in helping baptised persons grow in Christian faith and discipleship.

---

## 13. Baptism Within the Church Calendar

Baptism may be celebrated at appropriate times within the Church's worship calendar.

The current ACK Church Diary, Lectionary and diocesan directions should be consulted for:

* Appointed baptismal services
* Special baptismal occasions
* Liturgical seasons
* Related readings
* Local requirements

No particular date or season should be treated as universally required unless supported by an authoritative ACK source.

---

## 14. Parish and Diocesan Variation

Baptismal practice may vary in practical matters between dioceses and parishes.

Possible areas of variation include:

* Preparation classes
* Registration procedures
* Scheduling
* Number of candidates
* Parent and sponsor preparation
* Administrative requirements
* Local language
* Local pastoral arrangements

Such variations should be distinguished from the core authorised liturgical requirements of the Church.
