# Governance Records and Decisions

## 1. Overview

Governance records document the formal decisions, deliberations, resolutions, appointments, policies and other actions of the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK) at provincial level.

These records are important primary sources for understanding how the ACK is governed and how decisions have developed over time.

The ACK Hub should preserve governance records in a structured manner while clearly distinguishing:

* current decisions;
* historical decisions;
* constitutional provisions;
* Synod resolutions;
* committee decisions;
* recommendations;
* reports;
* appointments;
* amendments.

**Current-source verification:** September 2026.

---

## 2. Purpose of Governance Records

Governance records provide institutional evidence for:

* Church decisions;
* policy development;
* constitutional changes;
* appointments;
* elections;
* ministry direction;
* financial governance;
* institutional restructuring;
* establishment or dissolution of structures;
* strategic priorities;
* responses to significant issues;
* implementation of previous decisions.

Where an official primary document exists, it should be preferred over a secondary summary.

---

## 3. Major Sources of Governance Records

Important provincial governance records may originate from:

* Provincial Synod;
* House of Bishops;
* House of Clergy;
* House of Laity;
* Standing Committee of Provincial Synod;
* Provincial Boards;
* Provincial Commissions;
* Archbishop's Office;
* Provincial Secretariat;
* Provincial Archives.

The authority of a record depends on the body that issued it and the matter being addressed.

---

## 4. Provincial Synod Records

Provincial Synod records are among the most important governance records in the ACK.

They may include:

* Synod minutes;
* agendas;
* reports;
* resolutions;
* communiqués;
* Archbishop's Charges;
* committee reports;
* elections;
* appointments;
* constitutional amendments;
* strategic decisions.

Each record should retain its date and Synod session.

---

## 5. Standing Committee Records

Standing Committee records may include:

* meeting minutes;
* agendas;
* decisions;
* recommendations;
* reports;
* referrals;
* correspondence;
* appointment matters;
* matters referred to Provincial Synod.

A Standing Committee decision should not automatically be represented as a Provincial Synod resolution.

The record should identify the body that actually made the decision.

---

## 6. Provincial Board Records

Provincial Boards may produce governance and programme records relating to their areas of responsibility.

These may include:

### Board of Mission

* mission reports;
* evangelism reports;
* ministry decisions;
* programme reports;
* recommendations.

### Board of Social Services

* social transformation reports;
* development programme reports;
* research;
* justice and reconciliation work;
* communication matters.

### Board of Education and Training

* education reports;
* theological education matters;
* institutional reports;
* training decisions;
* educational recommendations.

### Board of Finance

* budget matters;
* financial reports;
* investment matters;
* stewardship decisions;
* financial recommendations.

Board records should be classified according to their formal status.

---

## 7. Constitutional Documents

Constitutional documents establish the formal governance framework of the ACK.

The ACK Hub should preserve:

* current authorised Provincial Constitution;
* previous constitutional editions;
* amendments;
* regulations;
* relevant schedules;
* constitutional interpretations where officially documented.

Each constitutional document should include metadata such as:

* title;
* version;
* publication date;
* effective date;
* amendment date;
* source;
* status.

The latest authorised version should be used for current constitutional questions.

---

## 8. Resolutions

A resolution is a formal decision recorded by the relevant governance body.

For every resolution added to the knowledge base, record:

| Field                    | Description                                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Resolution title/subject | What the resolution concerns               |
| Issuing body             | Body that passed the resolution            |
| Date                     | Date adopted                               |
| Session/meeting          | Relevant Synod or meeting                  |
| Resolution number        | Where available                            |
| Text/summary             | Accurate representation of the resolution  |
| Status                   | Current, historical, superseded or unclear |
| Source                   | Original document                          |

A resolution should not be separated from its source context.

---

## 9. Decisions

Not every governance decision is a Provincial Synod resolution.

A decision may be made by:

* Provincial Synod;
* Standing Committee;
* House of Bishops;
* Provincial Board;
* authorised officer;
* another body with delegated authority.

The knowledge base should identify the decision-making body.

The word **"decision"** should therefore not automatically imply that the Provincial Synod made the decision.

---

## 10. Recommendations

A recommendation is a proposal submitted for consideration by a competent authority.

Examples may include:

* Board recommendation to Standing Committee;
* Commission recommendation to a Board;
* Standing Committee recommendation to Provincial Synod;
* committee recommendation concerning appointments or policy.

A recommendation should not be presented as an approved policy unless the relevant authority subsequently adopted it.

---

## 11. Reports

Reports provide information, analysis or recommendations to a governance body.

They may cover:

* ministry activities;
* finances;
* education;
* social transformation;
* mission;
* institutional performance;
* strategic plans;
* programme implementation.

A report may contain recommendations without those recommendations having been adopted.

The knowledge base should therefore distinguish:

```text id="k7p3az"
REPORT
   │
   ├── Findings
   ├── Recommendations
   │
   ▼
Governance Body
   │
   └── Decision / Resolution
```

---

## 12. Minutes

Minutes provide an official record of a meeting or session.

Where available, the ACK Hub should preserve:

* meeting name;
* date;
* location;
* participants;
* agenda;
* matters discussed;
* decisions;
* resolutions;
* actions;
* adjournment/closing information.

Minutes should be treated as primary records where they are formally issued or authorised.

A summary of minutes should not replace the original document where the original is available.

---

## 13. Archbishop's Charges

The Archbishop's Charge is an important source for understanding provincial Church priorities and the Archbishop's address to the Church.

Charges may contain:

* reflections on the Church's ministry;
* strategic priorities;
* social concerns;
* mission priorities;
* institutional matters;
* calls to action;
* reports on developments.

An Archbishop's Charge should be stored with its date and relevant Synod/session context.

A Charge should not automatically be treated as a constitutional amendment or formal Synod resolution.

---

## 14. Appointments and Elections

Governance records may document:

* episcopal elections;
* provincial appointments;
* committee appointments;
* Board appointments;
* elections of officers;
* institutional appointments.

For each appointment or election, record:

* person;
* office;
* appointing/electing body;
* date;
* term;
* effective date;
* source;
* status.

Distinguish between:

**elected**

**appointed**

**consecrated**

**enthroned**

**assumed office**

These events may occur at different times.

---

## 15. Constitutional Amendments

Constitutional amendments are formal changes to the governing framework of the Church.

When recording an amendment, include:

* affected constitutional provision;
* amendment date;
* approving body;
* amendment text or accurate summary;
* effective date;
* previous wording where legally appropriate;
* current wording;
* source.

The current authorised Constitution should be treated as the controlling source for current constitutional questions.

---

## 16. Historical Governance Records

Historical records are essential to understanding the development of the ACK.

Historical materials may include:

* earlier Provincial Synod minutes;
* former constitutions;
* historical resolutions;
* previous Archbishop's Charges;
* former Board structures;
* former committee structures;
* historical appointments;
* archival correspondence.

Historical information should be retained rather than deleted simply because it is no longer current.

It must, however, be clearly labelled as historical.

---

## 17. Superseded Decisions

A decision may later be:

* amended;
* replaced;
* rescinded;
* superseded;
* incorporated into a newer policy;
* rendered obsolete by a constitutional change.

The ACK Hub should therefore avoid treating every historical decision as permanently applicable.

Where a later document changes an earlier decision, create a relationship such as:

```text id="z5n2qp"
Earlier Decision
      │
      ▼
Amended / Replaced / Superseded
      │
      ▼
Later Decision
```

---

## 18. Governance Record Metadata

Every governance record should ideally contain the following metadata:

```text id="m2x9vc"
Title:
Record Type:
Issuing Body:
Date:
Session / Meeting:
Reference Number:
Status:
Effective Date:
Supersedes:
Superseded By:
Source:
Verification Date:
Notes:
```

Not every field will be available for every document.

Missing information should be marked **"Not available"** or **"To be verified"** rather than guessed.

---

## 19. Source Hierarchy

When answering governance questions, the AI should generally prioritise sources in this order:

### Tier 1 — Authoritative primary sources

* authorised ACK Constitution;
* official Provincial Synod resolutions;
* official minutes;
* official appointment/election records;
* official governance documents.

### Tier 2 — Official ACK institutional sources

* ACK official website;
* official ACK archives;
* official ACK publications;
* official ACK announcements.

### Tier 3 — Secondary sources

* academic publications;
* books;
* reputable news reports;
* institutional research;
* other credible secondary material.

Secondary sources may provide context but should not override a more authoritative primary ACK record.

---

## 20. Conflicting Sources

Conflicting information should be explicitly flagged.

For example:

```text id="8s1f7q"
Source A
   │
   ├── Information X
   │
Source B
   │
   └── Information Y
```

The AI should:

1. identify the conflict;
2. check dates;
3. identify which source has greater authority;
4. prefer the newer authoritative source where appropriate;
5. avoid silently combining incompatible information;
6. flag unresolved conflicts.

A conflict between two official webpages should not be hidden.

---

## 21. Current versus Historical Status

Every governance record should ideally have one of these statuses:

* **Current**
* **Historical**
* **Superseded**
* **Amended**
* **Pending**
* **Unclear**
* **To be verified**

The status should be based on documentary evidence.

---

## 22. AI Retrieval Rules

When answering a question about an ACK governance decision, the AI should:

1. identify the relevant governance body;
2. locate the original record where possible;
3. check the date;
4. determine whether the record is current or historical;
5. identify later amendments or superseding decisions;
6. distinguish decisions from recommendations;
7. distinguish reports from resolutions;
8. provide the source;
9. avoid presenting assumptions as constitutional facts;
10. state uncertainty when the source record is incomplete.

---

## 23. Example Questions

### Question:

What did the Provincial Synod decide on a particular issue?

### Retrieval approach:

```text id="4r6k2m"
Identify issue
      ↓
Find Provincial Synod session
      ↓
Find minutes / resolution
      ↓
Check later amendments
      ↓
Return documented decision + source
```

---

### Question:

Is an old Synod resolution still applicable?

### Retrieval approach:

Check:

1. original resolution;
2. later Synod resolutions;
3. constitutional amendments;
4. relevant current policies;
5. official ACK clarification.

Do not assume that age alone makes a resolution invalid, and do not assume that an old resolution remains current without checking subsequent records.

---

### Question:

Who made the decision?

The AI should identify the actual issuing or decision-making body rather than simply attributing the decision to "ACK."

---

## 24. Records Management Principles

Governance records should be:

* accurately named;
* dated;
* sourced;
* version-controlled;
* classified;
* preserved;
* searchable;
* linked to related structures;
* protected against accidental alteration.

Where a document is replaced, the previous version should normally remain available as a historical record unless there is a valid reason for removal.

---

## 25. Sensitive Information

Some governance records may contain confidential or sensitive information.

The ACK Hub should follow applicable:

* Church confidentiality requirements;
* data-protection requirements;
* legal requirements;
* document-access restrictions.

Sensitive personal information should not be exposed merely because it appears in an internal governance document.

Public-facing AI responses should respect the access classification of the source.
