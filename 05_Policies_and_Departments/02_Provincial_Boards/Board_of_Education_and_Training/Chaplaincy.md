# ACK Chaplaincy – Board of Education and Training

## Overview

Chaplaincy is a ministry function that appears within the Anglican Church of Kenya's education and training structure as well as its wider mission work.

The ACK Board of Education and Training identifies a **Theological Education by Extension (TEE) & Chaplaincy Committee**. The Board's education mandate includes the development and support of chaplaincy, including ministry in schools and other institutions.

Chaplaincy should therefore be understood as a cross-cutting ministry area rather than as an ordinary academic department.

## Chaplaincy and the Board of Education and Training

Within the education and training context, chaplaincy contributes to:

* Christian ministry in educational institutions.
* Pastoral and spiritual support.
* Christian formation.
* Training and development of chaplains.
* Support for learners and institutional communities.
* Extension of the Church's ministry into public and private institutions.

The ACK's education directorate has historically supported chaplaincy alongside theological education, liturgical development, capacity building, publishing, documentation and archiving.

## TEE & Chaplaincy Committee

The ACK Decade Strategy 2018–2027 identifies a **TEE & Chaplaincy Committee** under the Provincial Board of Education & Training.

The strategy also identifies Chaplaincy Committee separately under the Board of Mission. This shows that chaplaincy has historically had responsibilities spanning both education/training and mission structures.

For the ACK Hub, these structures should be preserved as separate governance references rather than merged into one department.

## Chaplaincy in Specialized Ministry

ACK chaplaincy extends Christian ministry into settings where people may not ordinarily participate in parish-based ministry.

ACK sources describe chaplaincy work in areas including:

* Schools and learning institutions.
* Hospitals and health facilities.
* Prisons.
* Airports and ports.
* Disciplined and uniformed forces.
* Other public and professional environments.

The ACK has particularly emphasized chaplaincy among the disciplined and uniformed forces.

## Relationship with Theological Education

Chaplaincy and theological education are related but distinct.

**Theological Education by Extension (TEE)** focuses primarily on theological and ministry education through an extension-based model.

**Chaplaincy** focuses primarily on applying Christian ministry and pastoral care within specialized institutional settings.

Training and theological formation can therefore support chaplaincy, while chaplaincy provides practical ministry contexts in which trained church workers serve.

## Relationship with the Board of Mission

The Board of Mission currently identifies Provincial Chaplaincy as part of its wider mission structure. The current ACK Board of Mission page lists the Provincial Chaplaincy Chairman and Chaplaincy Secretary among its leadership.

## Diocesan Implementation

Chaplaincy is also implemented at diocesan level.

Diocesan structures may include senior chaplains, hospital chaplains, prisons chaplains, school chaplains and other specialized ministry positions.

For example, current ACK diocesan information records diocesan chaplaincy positions alongside other diocesan officers and departments.

The ACK Hub should record diocesan chaplaincy structures separately from the provincial governance structure.
