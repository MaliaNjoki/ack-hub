# ACK Provincial Theological Education

## Overview

Provincial Theological Education is a core function of the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK) Board of Education and Training.

Its purpose is to provide theological, ministerial and Christian formation for people preparing for ordained ministry, lay ministry, church leadership and other forms of Christian service. Theological education is also connected to the wider educational mission of the ACK and its commitment to equipping people for service in the Church and society.

The ACK's current Board of Education and Training brings together Provincial Theological Education with other education-related functions, including the Provincial Colleges Council, Education, Liturgical work, Theological Education by Extension (TEE), Chaplaincy and Archives.

---

## Historical Development

The ACK has a long history of theological and ministerial training.

According to the ACK's official account, theological training in the Anglican Church in Kenya can be traced to the early missionary period. In **1889**, evangelists and church teachers began training at the Divinity School at Freretown under the leadership of Mr. Fitch.

The training developed over time to respond to the growing need for African clergy, evangelists, teachers and church leaders.

The ACK's theological-training institutions subsequently expanded, providing different levels and forms of theological education.

The development of theological education therefore forms an important part of the historical development of the Anglican Church in Kenya.

## Main Purpose

Provincial Theological Education supports the ACK by:

* Preparing candidates for ordained ministry.
* Providing theological and biblical education.
* Developing pastoral and ministry skills.
* Strengthening Christian leadership.
* Supporting Anglican identity and tradition.
* Equipping clergy and other church workers for effective ministry.
* Providing opportunities for continuing theological formation.
* Supporting the academic and spiritual development of students.
* Contributing to the wider educational mission of the Church.

---

## Divinity and Theological Training

Divinity training is one of the historical foundations of theological education within the ACK.

The training has developed from early programmes for evangelists and church teachers into broader theological programmes serving candidates for ordained ministry and other Christian workers.

The ACK's current educational institutions include institutions that provide theological, pastoral, Anglican and related training at different levels.

Theological training may include areas such as:

* Biblical studies.
* Theology.
* Church history.
* Anglican studies.
* Pastoral studies.
* Christian ministry.
* Evangelism and mission.
* Worship and liturgy.
* Christian leadership.
* Practical ministry.
* Ethics and Christian formation.

The exact programmes and qualifications depend on the individual institution.

## Provincial Theological Institutions

The ACK's official institutions information identifies several educational institutions connected with Anglican theological and ministerial training.

Examples include:

* **St. Paul's University, Limuru**.
* **St. Andrew's College of Theology & Development, Kabare**.
* **Berea Christian College for Integrated Studies**.
* **St. Paul's Theological College – Kapsabet**.
* **St. Philip's Theological College, Maseno**.
* **Bishop Okullu College of Theology and Development, Bondo**.
* **ACK Language and Orientation School**.

These institutions differ in their history, ownership arrangements, programmes and institutional status. They should therefore not automatically be treated as identical types of provincial theological colleges.

## Relationship with the Provincial Colleges Council

The Provincial Colleges Council is another education-related function within the ACK Board of Education and Training.

The Council provides a provincial framework through which Anglican educational and theological institutions can be coordinated within the Church's wider education and training structure.

Provincial Theological Education and the Provincial Colleges Council should therefore be treated as related but distinct knowledge-base categories:

* **Provincial Theological Education** — the theological and ministerial education function.
* **Provincial Colleges Council** — the coordinating/governance function relating to provincial colleges and institutions.

## Theological Formation

Theological education within the ACK is not limited to academic study.

It also contributes to the formation of people preparing for Christian ministry.

This includes development in areas such as:

* Christian character.
* Spiritual formation.
* Biblical understanding.
* Anglican identity.
* Pastoral responsibility.
* Leadership.
* Worship.
* Mission and evangelism.
* Service to the Church and wider community.

The combination of academic, spiritual and practical formation is important because theological education prepares people not only to acquire knowledge but also to serve within the ministry and mission of the Church.

---

## Relationship with Ordained Ministry

Theological education plays an important role in preparing candidates for ordained ministry.

The ACK's historical account explains that theological institutions developed partly in response to the need to train African clergy and church leaders. Several of the Church's theological institutions continue to provide training relevant to ordained and other forms of Christian ministry.

Theological education should therefore be understood as part of the wider pathway of ministerial formation rather than simply as general academic education.

---

## Relationship with Anglican Tradition

Theological education also supports understanding and transmission of Anglican identity and tradition.

Relevant areas may include:

* Anglican doctrine and tradition.
* Anglican history.
* Liturgy and worship.
* Biblical interpretation.
* Sacramental theology.
* Pastoral ministry.
* Mission and evangelism.
* Christian ethics.

This connects theological education with other functions of the Board of Education and Training, particularly **Liturgy**, **TEE**, **Education** and **Chaplaincy**.

---

## Theological Education by Extension (TEE)

Theological Education by Extension is a related but distinct area of the ACK's education and training structure.

TEE provides theological learning through an extension-based approach rather than requiring every learner to undertake residential college-based education.

For the ACK knowledge base:

> **Provincial Theological Education** should describe the broader theological-education function, while **Theological Education by Extension (TEE)** should have its own dedicated file describing its specific structure and delivery model.

## Relationship with Other ACK Structures

Provincial Theological Education operates within the wider governance and ministry structure of the Anglican Church of Kenya.

It has relationships with:

* The Board of Education and Training.
* Provincial Colleges Council.
* Education function.
* Liturgy function.
* Theological Education by Extension.
* Chaplaincy.

## Provincial and Diocesan Levels

The ACK's education system operates at different levels.

### Provincial Level

At the provincial level, the Board of Education and Training provides oversight and coordination for the Church's education and training functions.

### Diocesan Level

Dioceses may have their own:

* Education departments.
* Theological training programmes.
* TEE programmes.
* Education coordinators.
* Diocesan colleges or training centres.
* Clergy and lay training programmes.

These diocesan structures should be recorded separately from provincial structures in the knowledge base.

For example, the ACK's diocesan information pages identify education and TEE functions within individual dioceses.
