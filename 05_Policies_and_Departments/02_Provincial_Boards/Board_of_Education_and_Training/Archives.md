# ACK Provincial Archives

## Overview

The **ACK Provincial Archives** is the archival agency responsible for preserving and providing access to the historical heritage of the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

The Archives forms part of the education and training structure of the ACK and is specifically concerned with the preservation, management and accessibility of Anglican records and historical materials.

The ACK describes the Archives as responsible for providing permanent care for Anglican heritage in Kenya and for ensuring sound records-management practices within the Province.

---

## Purpose

The primary purpose of the Provincial Archives is to ensure that records and other materials of enduring historical value are properly identified, collected, preserved, organised and made accessible.

Its work supports:

* Preservation of Anglican heritage.
* Records management.
* Historical research.
* Institutional memory.
* Documentation of the development of the Church.
* Access to information.
* Research and education.
* Understanding the history and development of the ACK.

The Archives therefore serves both as a preservation institution and as a research and reference resource.

---

## Historical Background

The ACK identifies the history of archival work in Kenya with the arrival and work of the **Church Missionary Society (CMS)**.

The official ACK Archives page states that archival work dates back to **1844**, when CMS began its work in Kenya.

The Church preserved records documenting its missionary, educational, administrative and social activities. These records subsequently became important sources for understanding the development of the Anglican Church and aspects of Kenya's wider history.

The collection therefore extends beyond purely ecclesiastical matters and contains material relating to communities, institutions, education, healthcare, leadership and other aspects of historical development.

## Archival Collection

The ACK Provincial Archives states that its collection contains **more than 7,000 folders**, with material dating back to **1844**.

The collection includes some of the earliest surviving records associated with Anglican activity in Kenya, including:

* Early burial records.
* Church Missionary Society records.
* Correspondence.
* Birth and marriage registers.
* Minutes of church bodies.
* Early church-planting records.
* Church institutional records.
* Healthcare records.
* Records of church organisations.
* Leadership and structural records.
* Records relating to historical events and developments.

The collection therefore provides a significant source of information for research into the history of the ACK and its wider social context.

## Records Management

The Archives is not concerned only with old historical documents.

The official ACK mandate also includes supporting **sound records-management practices in the Province**.

Records management involves appropriate handling of records throughout their lifecycle, including:

* Creation.
* Use.
* Maintenance.
* Storage.
* Transfer.
* Preservation.
* Disposal where authorised.

The Archives policy objectives specifically refer to best practices in the creation, use, preservation, transfer and disposal of records.

This makes the Archives relevant to the broader information-management system of the ACK.

---

## Identification and Acquisition

The Provincial Archives identifies and acquires materials that have lasting historical or institutional value.

This includes records that document:

* The growth of the Church.
* Development of dioceses.
* Church leadership.
* Missionary activity.
* Christian ministry.
* Education.
* Healthcare.
* Church organisations.
* Church institutions.
* Community engagement.

The Archives may also receive private records where their historical value makes them important to the Anglican heritage of Kenya.

---

## Arrangement and Description

Archival records must be organised so that they can be located and understood by users.

The ACK states that archival materials are arranged and described according to archival principles.

This supports:

* Retrieval of records.
* Preservation of context.
* Research.
* Reference services.
* Institutional memory.

For the ACK knowledge base, archival descriptions should preserve the relationship between a record and the institution, person, event or activity that created it.

---

## Preservation

Preservation is a central responsibility of the Provincial Archives.

The Archives policy identifies the need to:

* Provide appropriate storage conditions.
* Protect archival collections.
* Preserve records of intrinsic or original value.
* Maintain appropriate security.
* Use suitable preservation facilities.
* Protect historical monuments and related heritage.

The ACK policy specifically notes that records of intrinsic/original value should be appropriately preserved and stored, including use of fire-resistant storage where required.

## Relationship with the Board of Education and Training

The Archives is included within the ACK's education and training framework.

The official Board of Education page identifies the **Archives Department** among the departments supporting the Church's engagement in education and related functions. The same page states that the Archives seeks to preserve Anglican material for posterity through maintenance of records.

The ACK's current Boards page describes the Board of Education and Training as bringing together Provincial Theological Education, Provincial Colleges Council, Education and Liturgical Council.

The Archives therefore represents a specialised information-preservation function within the wider education and training structure.

---

## Relationship with the Provincial Secretariat

The Archives also interacts with the wider provincial administration.

The official Archives policy states that some archival matters may be determined by the **Provincial Secretary**.

This reflects the relationship between records management, provincial administration and archival preservation.

Examples include:

## Relationship with Liturgical Materials

The Archives preserves historical materials connected with Anglican worship.

These may include:

* Older prayer books.
* Hymn books.
* Lectionaries.
* Service materials.
* Church registers.
* Historical worship documents.

The Archives therefore complements the **Liturgical** function by preserving historical editions and records, while the Liturgical function focuses on worship and liturgical development.

---

## Relationship with Publishing

The Archives also has a relationship with the preservation of published Church materials.

ACK **Uzima Publishing House** has historically published prayer books, hymn books, church registers, lectionaries and other Christian literature. Historical editions of such materials may have archival value.

The knowledge base should distinguish:

* **Publishing** — production and distribution of materials.
* **Archives** — preservation and access to records of enduring value.

## Digital Records and the Knowledge Base

Modern records may exist in digital as well as physical form.

For the ACK knowledge base, digital records should be treated according to their information value and institutional context.

Where appropriate, digital archival information should preserve:

* Original creator.
* Date.
* Document title.
* Institution.
* Record type.
* Version.
* Location.
* Access restrictions.
* Preservation status.

Digitisation should not be assumed to eliminate the importance of the original archival record.

## Access Restrictions and Data Quality

Not every archival record should automatically be treated as publicly accessible.

The official Archives policy states that access may be restricted by legal requirements or written agreements with donors.

For the knowledge base:

* Do not expose restricted records as publicly available information.
* Do not infer private information from archival material.
* Preserve access restrictions where documented.
* Distinguish public records from restricted records.
* Record provenance where possible.
* Cite the archival source when using historical claims.
* Preserve the date or period of the record.
