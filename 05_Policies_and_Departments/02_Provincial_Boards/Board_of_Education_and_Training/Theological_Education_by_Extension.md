# ACK Theological Education by Extension (TEE)

## Overview

Theological Education by Extension (TEE) is an education and ministry-training function within the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK) Board of Education and Training.

TEE provides theological and ministry education through an extension-based approach, allowing church leaders and other Christian workers to receive training while continuing to serve within their local churches and communities.

The ACK officially identifies **Theological Education by Extension and Chaplaincy** among the departments/functions under the Board of Education and Training.

## Purpose of TEE

Theological Education by Extension supports the continuing development of people serving the Church by making theological and ministry training more accessible.

Its purposes include:

* Providing theological education beyond conventional residential college programmes.
* Equipping clergy, lay leaders and other church workers with relevant knowledge and ministry skills.
* Supporting continuing theological education for church leaders.
* Strengthening biblical understanding and Christian formation.
* Supporting effective ministry within local congregations and communities.
* Connecting theological learning with practical ministry.
* Expanding access to theological education across the Province.

## Extension-Based Learning

TEE differs from fully residential theological education because learning can be undertaken while participants continue their ministry and other responsibilities.

This approach can support learners who are:

* Serving in local churches.
* Working as lay leaders or church workers.
* Serving in remote or geographically dispersed areas.
* Unable to participate in full-time residential theological programmes.
* Seeking continuing theological and ministry education.

The extension model therefore provides a link between formal theological education and practical ministry at the local level.

## Areas of Study and Formation

TEE may cover areas relevant to Christian ministry and church leadership, including:

* Biblical studies.
* Christian theology.
* Anglican identity and tradition.
* Church history.
* Christian ethics.
* Pastoral ministry.
* Evangelism and mission.
* Christian leadership.
* Worship and liturgy.
* Community and church ministry.
* Practical ministry skills.
* Christian formation.

Specific programmes, curricula and qualifications should be recorded according to the relevant official ACK or accredited institutional documentation.

## Continuing Theological Education

TEE contributes to the continuing theological education of church leaders.

This is particularly important because theological formation does not necessarily end when a person enters ministry. Church leaders may require ongoing opportunities to strengthen their theological knowledge, ministry practice and understanding of changing social and community contexts.

The programme can therefore complement other forms of theological education provided through ACK-affiliated and related theological institutions.

## Relationship with Theological Institutions

TEE is related to the wider ACK theological education system but should not automatically be treated as a theological college or university.

The ACK has a number of theological and educational institutions, including St. Paul's University, St. Andrew's College of Theology and Development, St. Philip's Theological College, Bishop Hannington Institute of Theology, St. Paul's Theological College Kapsabet, Berea Christian College for Integrated Studies and Bishop Okullu College.

TEE provides an extension-based pathway alongside these institutional forms of theological education.

## Provincial and Diocesan Implementation

TEE has both provincial and diocesan relevance.

At the provincial level, TEE is identified within the Board of Education and Training.

At diocesan level, TEE may operate through diocesan education structures, TEE coordinators, training centres or other locally established arrangements.

The ACK diocesan directory provides examples of dioceses with TEE structures, including diocesan TEE coordinators, TEE departments and TEE/lay-training centres.

This demonstrates that TEE is implemented within local diocesan contexts rather than functioning only as a centralized provincial programme.

## Relationship with Chaplaincy

The official ACK Board of Education structure places TEE and Chaplaincy together within the same committee/function.

However, TEE and Chaplaincy perform different roles:

**TEE**

* Focuses on theological and ministry education.
* Supports continuing theological formation.
* Provides extension-based learning opportunities.

**Chaplaincy**

* Focuses on Christian ministry and pastoral care in specialized settings.
* Includes ministry in institutions and sectors such as schools, health facilities, the military and other public or professional environments.

For the ACK Hub, Chaplaincy should therefore remain separately documented while being cross-referenced to this section.

## Historical and Strategic Context

The ACK's Decade Strategy 2018–2027 identifies the **Provincial TEE Programme** among the Province's educational institutions and programmes.

The same strategy identifies a **TEE & Chaplaincy Committee** under the Board of Education and Training.

This provides a useful historical and strategic reference for understanding the development and organization of TEE within the Province.

## Governance

TEE falls within the education and training mandate of the ACK.

The official ACK Board of Education page identifies the TEE and Chaplaincy function within the Board of Education and Training.

The current ACK Board page identifies the **Rt. Rev. Liverson Mng'onda** as chairperson of the TEE & Chaplaincy committee.

Current office-holder information should always be verified against the latest official ACK publication before being treated as permanent historical data.
