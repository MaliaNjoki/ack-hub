# ACK Board of Education and Training

## 1. Overview

The **Board of Education and Training** is one of the principal Provincial Boards of the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

The Board provides oversight and coordination for the Church's education and training responsibilities, including theological education, educational institutions, archives and related training functions.

Its work supports the development of clergy, Church workers, students and other members of the Anglican Church and contributes to the wider mission of the ACK.

## 2. Main Areas of Responsibility

The ACK identifies several areas within the work of the Board of Education and Training, including:

* Provincial Theological Education
* Provincial Colleges Council
* Education
* Liturgical
* Archives
* Theological Education by Extension (TEE)
* Chaplaincy

These should be understood as areas of responsibility or institutional functions unless an official ACK source specifically identifies a particular unit as a department.

## 3. Provincial Theological Education

The Board provides a provincial framework for theological education and training.

This area supports the preparation and continuing development of people serving the Church, including clergy and other Church workers.

Theological education may include:

* Biblical studies
* Theology
* Anglican studies
* Ministry formation
* Pastoral studies
* Christian leadership
* Practical ministry
* Continuing ministerial development

Specific institutions and programmes should be documented separately from the Board's governance structure.

## 4. Provincial Colleges Council

The **Provincial Colleges Council** relates to Anglican colleges and institutions involved in education and theological training.

The Council provides a framework for coordination and oversight of relevant Anglican educational institutions.

The ACK's educational institutions include theological colleges, universities and other specialised institutions.

An institution should not automatically be classified as a Provincial Board or department.

## 5. Education

Education is a major part of the ACK's ministry and institutional work.

The Church participates in education through:

* Schools
* Colleges
* Universities
* Theological institutions
* Specialised training institutions
* Christian education programmes

Education contributes to the Church's mission by supporting intellectual, spiritual, professional and leadership development.

## 6. Liturgical Function

The Board's identified responsibilities include a **Liturgical** area.

This connects education and training with the Church's understanding and practice of Anglican worship and liturgical life.

Relevant knowledge may include:

* Anglican worship
* Liturgical formation
* Worship leadership
* Liturgical education
* Approved liturgical resources
* Training related to worship and ministry

The ACK Hub should cross-reference detailed liturgical material stored elsewhere in the repository rather than duplicating entire liturgical documents here.

## 7. Archives

Archives form part of the Board's identified areas of responsibility.

The archival function contributes to the preservation and management of the historical and institutional records of the Anglican Church of Kenya.

Relevant archival materials may include:

* Historical Church records
* Institutional records
* Photographs
* Publications
* Correspondence
* Administrative records
* Historical reports
* Other documents of enduring institutional value

Archival information is particularly important for establishing historical facts about the ACK.

## 8. Theological Education by Extension (TEE)

**Theological Education by Extension (TEE)** provides a flexible approach to theological education.

It can support people who require theological training while remaining in their existing ministry, community or work environment.

TEE can contribute to:

* Biblical knowledge
* Theological understanding
* Christian ministry
* Lay training
* Leadership development
* Continuing theological education

Specific TEE programmes, curricula and centres should be documented using the relevant official ACK sources.

## 9. Chaplaincy

Chaplaincy is identified among the Board's education and training-related areas.

Chaplaincy involves specialised Christian ministry in settings where people may not have normal access to parish-based Church life.

Relevant settings may include:

* Schools and learning institutions
* Hospitals
* Prisons
* Disciplined and uniformed services
* Airports
* Ports

The ACK also has a wider Chaplaincy function associated with the Board of Mission.

For the ACK Hub, chaplaincy information should therefore be cross-referenced between the relevant Provincial Boards rather than duplicated unnecessarily.

## 10. Educational Institutions

The ACK has a number of educational and theological institutions associated with its education and training work.

Examples identified by the ACK include:

* St. Paul's University
* St. Philip's Theological College
* Bishop Okullu College
* ACK Language and Orientation School

These are **institutions**, not individual departments of the Board.

Each institution should eventually have its own knowledge-base file where sufficient authoritative information is available.

## 11. Relationship to Provincial Governance

The Board of Education and Training operates within the wider governance framework of the Anglican Church of Kenya.

The Board should be understood as a **Provincial Board**, alongside:

* Board of Mission
* Board of Social Services
* Board of Finance

Its work contributes to the wider strategic and ministerial objectives of the ACK.

## 12. Relationship with Other Boards

Education and training naturally overlap with other areas of Church ministry.

Examples include:

**Board of Education and Training ↔ Board of Mission**

Through theological education, ministry formation, liturgical training and chaplaincy.

**Board of Education and Training ↔ Board of Social Services**

Through education, social development, community training and institutional capacity development.

**Board of Education and Training ↔ Board of Finance**

Through financial administration and oversight of educational institutions and programmes.

These relationships do not mean that the Boards are merged; they reflect areas where their responsibilities intersect.
