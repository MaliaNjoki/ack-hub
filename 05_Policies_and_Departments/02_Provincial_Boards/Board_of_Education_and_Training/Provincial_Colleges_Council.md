# ACK Provincial Colleges Council

## Overview

The **Provincial Colleges Council** is one of the education and training functions brought together under the **Board of Education and Training** of the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

The Council relates to the coordination and oversight of Anglican educational and theological college structures within the wider provincial education and training framework.

The ACK's current Board of Education and Training brings together **Provincial Theological Education, Provincial Colleges Council, Education and Liturgical Council**.

---

## Purpose

The Provincial Colleges Council forms part of the ACK's provincial framework for coordinating education and training institutions.

Its broad purposes include:

* Supporting the development of Anglican educational institutions.
* Providing coordination within the provincial college system.
* Supporting theological and ministerial training.
* Promoting appropriate educational standards.
* Connecting educational institutions with the wider mission of the Church.
* Supporting the development of Christian leaders and church workers.
* Providing a framework for collaboration among relevant institutions.

The Council should be understood as a **coordination and governance function**, rather than as a single physical college.

---

## Historical Development

The development of Anglican colleges in Kenya is closely connected to the history of theological education in the Church.

Early Anglican education and theological training developed through missionary institutions and training centres. The ACK's institutional history records the development of theological education from early Divinity School work into a wider network of colleges and educational institutions.

Over time, different institutions developed different roles, including:

* Training evangelists.
* Training clergy and candidates for ordained ministry.
* Providing theological education.
* Providing pastoral training.
* Providing Christian leadership training.
* Providing professional and other educational programmes.

The provincial college framework therefore developed alongside the expansion of the ACK's education and training mission.

---

## Relationship with Theological Education

The Provincial Colleges Council is closely related to **Provincial Theological Education**, but the two should remain separate knowledge-base concepts.

### Provincial Theological Education

Focuses on the broader theological and ministerial education function of the ACK.

### Provincial Colleges Council

Focuses on the coordination and institutional framework associated with provincial colleges and related educational institutions.

This distinction is important when structuring the ACK knowledge base.

---

## Examples of Institutional Roles

### St. Paul's University

St. Paul's University developed from the Anglican theological education tradition and became a chartered private university in 2007.

The ACK is identified on the official ACK institutions page as the university's major shareholder, with other participating church bodies also holding shares. The university offers programmes including theology, communication, community development, business, information technology and health.

### St. Paul's Theological College – Kapsabet

The institution was established in 1963 as the Kapsabet Church Training Centre.

The ACK records that it became one of the provincial colleges in 1993 and provides training in areas including theology, pastoral studies and Anglicanism.

### St. Andrew's College of Theology & Development, Kabare

The college was established by the late Archbishop David Gitari after the creation of the Diocese of Mt. Kenya East.

The institution provides theological, professional and artisan courses and has a particular emphasis on preparing people for holistic Christian mission.

### Berea Christian College for Integrated Studies

The institution began as Berea Theological College in 1985.

The ACK records that it was recommended by the provincial Board of Theological Education to become one of the provincial colleges and subsequently adopted a theological training curriculum leading to diploma-level training.

### Bishop Hannington Institute

The institution has roots in early Anglican theological training in Mombasa.

The ACK records the development of Divinity training at Freretown and later the establishment and development of the institution in Mombasa. Its programmes have included Biblical Studies, Historical Studies, Theological Studies and Religious Studies.

### St. Philip's Theological College

St. Philip's Theological College is listed by the ACK among its educational institutions and is located at Maseno.

### Bishop Okullu College of Theology and Development

Bishop Okullu College of Theology and Development is listed among ACK educational institutions and is located in the Bondo area.

## Institutional Diversity

ACK educational institutions do not all perform the same function.

They may include:

* Universities.
* Theological colleges.
* Bible and ministry training institutions.
* Professional training institutions.
* Language and orientation institutions.
* Institutions providing programmes validated or delivered in partnership with other institutions.

Consequently, the knowledge base should preserve the specific identity and institutional status of each organisation.

---

## Relationship with the Board of Education and Training

The Provincial Colleges Council operates within the wider **Board of Education and Training** structure.

The Board's education mandate seeks to use teaching and education to reduce ignorance and contribute to human development, the Church and society. The Board brings together several education-related functions, including the Provincial Colleges Council.

For the knowledge base, the hierarchy can therefore be represented as:

**ACK Provincial Structure**
→ **Board of Education and Training**
→ **Provincial Colleges Council**
→ **Relevant educational and theological institutions**

This should not be interpreted as meaning that every institution is directly managed by the Council.

---

## Relationship with Dioceses

The ACK operates through both provincial and diocesan structures.

Dioceses may have their own education departments, theological training programmes, institutions and other educational initiatives.

These should be recorded separately from the provincial college structure unless an official source establishes a direct relationship.

The ACK's diocesan information demonstrates that education and training activities can exist at diocesan level alongside provincial educational structures.

---

## Relationship with Other Education Functions

The Provincial Colleges Council is connected to several other areas within the Board of Education and Training.

### Provincial Theological Education

Provides the broader theological and ministerial education framework.

### Education

Covers the wider educational mission of the Church.

### Liturgical Council

Relates to liturgical education, worship and Anglican formation.

### Theological Education by Extension

Provides an extension-based approach to theological learning and should be documented as a separate knowledge-base area.

### Chaplaincy

Theological and professional training can support chaplaincy ministry in schools, hospitals, institutions of higher learning and disciplined forces.

ACK strategic material has also identified theological colleges and training institutions as important for developing chaplains and other ministry personnel.

---

## Institutional Governance and Partnerships

ACK educational institutions may have different ownership, affiliation, partnership and validation arrangements.

For example, the official ACK institutions page records that St. Paul's University has multiple participating church bodies and that Carlile College has relationships with other educational institutions for validation of programmes.

The knowledge base should therefore distinguish:

* ACK ownership.
* ACK affiliation.
* Institutional partnership.
* Programme validation.
* Ecumenical participation.
* Diocesan ownership or sponsorship.
* Provincial recognition or coordination.

These categories should not be treated as interchangeable.

---

## Historical vs Current Information

Historical institutional information should be preserved separately from current institutional status.

For example, an institution may have:

* Changed its name.
* Changed its institutional status.
* Expanded its programmes.
* Changed its ownership arrangements.
* Developed partnerships with other churches or institutions.

Historical facts should therefore be labelled with their relevant period rather than presented as current information.

## Data Quality Notes

For the ACK knowledge base:

* Treat the Provincial Colleges Council as a **provincial function**, not as a physical college.
* Do not automatically classify every ACK educational institution as being directly managed by the Council.
* Record each institution separately where detailed institutional information is required.
* Distinguish universities from theological colleges and other training institutions.
* Distinguish current institutional status from historical status.
* Record ownership, affiliation and partnership arrangements separately.
* Verify current institutional leadership and programmes from official institutional sources before recording them.
* Do not assume that a diocesan college or training centre is a provincial institution.
* Preserve the relationship between the Council and the wider Board of Education and Training.
