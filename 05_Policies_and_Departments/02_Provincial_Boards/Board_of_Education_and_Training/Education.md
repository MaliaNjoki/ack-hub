# ACK Education

## Overview

**Education** is one of the principal functions within the **Board of Education and Training** of the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

The ACK has historically and currently participated in the education sector through schools, educational institutions, support for learners, production of educational and Christian materials, training of clergy and deployment of chaplains to educational institutions.

The ACK describes education as part of its wider mission of reducing ignorance and promoting the advancement of the Church and society.

---

## Purpose

The ACK education function seeks to contribute to the holistic development of individuals through education and Christian formation.

Its broad purposes include:

* Promoting access to education.
* Supporting educational institutions.
* Contributing to Christian formation within education.
* Supporting learners who need educational assistance.
* Developing teachers, clergy and other education-related personnel.
* Providing educational and Christian literature.
* Supporting Anglican identity and values within appropriate educational settings.
* Linking education with the wider mission of the Church.
* Contributing to human development and service to society.

---

## Historical Development

The Anglican Church has been involved in education in Kenya since the early missionary period.

Missionary work included the establishment of schools and training centres alongside evangelistic activities. Early Anglican education was closely connected with literacy, Christian instruction, leadership development and preparation of people for service in the Church and wider society.

The ACK's historical account of its education and theological-training work records the development of institutions from early missionary initiatives into a wider network of educational and theological institutions.

Education subsequently expanded beyond basic schooling to include:

* Secondary education.
* Teacher education.
* Theological education.
* University education.
* Professional training.
* Language and orientation programmes.
* Christian leadership development.
* Chaplaincy and institutional ministry.

---

## Education within the Board of Education and Training

The current ACK Board of Education and Training brings together several education-related functions.

These include:

* Provincial Theological Education.
* Provincial Colleges Council.
* Education.
* Liturgical Council.

The Board also has committees and related functions dealing with areas such as Theological Education by Extension, Chaplaincy, Archives and Liturgy.

For the knowledge base, **Education** should therefore be treated as a distinct functional area within the wider Board.

---

## Schools and Educational Institutions

The ACK has participated in education through schools and other educational institutions.

Its educational involvement has included:

* Establishment and support of schools.
* Christian education.
* Support for needy students.
* Development of educational institutions.
* Training and formation of teachers and church workers.
* Chaplaincy within schools and other institutions.
* Production of educational and Christian materials.

The current ACK institutions information also identifies a number of educational and theological institutions that are owned by, affiliated with, or associated with the Church.

Examples include:

* St. Paul's University.
* St. Philip's Theological College.
* Bishop Okullu College of Theology and Development.
* ACK Language and Orientation School.

These institutions have different histories and institutional purposes and should therefore be documented individually where detailed information is required.

---

## Support for Needy Students

The ACK identifies support for the education of needy students as part of its involvement in the education sector.

Such support forms part of the Church's wider concern for human development and access to educational opportunities.

For the knowledge base, student-support programmes should be recorded separately from the governance structure of the Education function where the programme has its own responsible department, institution or funding mechanism.

---

## Educational Materials

The ACK's education function also has a relationship with the production of educational and Christian materials.

These materials can support:

* Christian education.
* Anglican formation.
* Worship.
* Biblical learning.
* Church teaching.
* Educational institutions.
* Clergy and lay training.

The ACK identifies the production of materials such as hymn books, lectionaries and other Christian literature as part of its education-related work.

The ACK Uzima Publishing House is a related institutional example. The official ACK account states that Uzima Press was founded in 1974 to print and publish liturgical works for the Anglican Church in Kenya. Its governance includes representatives connected to the ACK education and training structure.

Education within the ACK context is connected to Christian formation.

## Chaplaincy and Education

Chaplaincy provides an important connection between education and ministry.

The ACK's Board of Education and Training includes Chaplaincy within its wider education and training structure.

Chaplaincy can provide Christian ministry and pastoral support in settings such as:

* Schools.
* Universities and colleges.
* Health institutions.
* Military institutions.
* Other public and private institutions.

The Board's official description specifically connects chaplaincy with providing Christian models of service in public and private sectors.

## Relationship with Theological Education

General education and theological education are related but distinct.

### Education

Covers the wider educational mission of the Church, including schools, educational support, educational materials and broader human development.

### Provincial Theological Education

Focuses specifically on theological, ministerial and Christian leadership formation.

The two functions operate within the wider Board of Education and Training.

---

## Relationship with Provincial Colleges Council

The **Provincial Colleges Council** is another distinct function within the Board.

Education and the Provincial Colleges Council are related because educational institutions form part of the Church's wider education system.

However:

* **Education** describes the broader education function.
* **Provincial Colleges Council** describes the coordination and institutional framework associated with relevant colleges and educational institutions.

They should therefore remain separate files in the ACK knowledge base.

---

## Provincial and Diocesan Education

Education exists at both provincial and diocesan levels.

### Provincial Level

The Provincial Board of Education and Training provides the wider provincial framework for education and training.

### Diocesan Level

Individual dioceses may have their own:

* Education departments.
* Education secretaries or coordinators.
* Schools.
* Training centres.
* Scholarship or student-support programmes.
* Chaplaincy programmes.
* TEE programmes.

The current ACK diocesan information demonstrates that Education Departments and education officers can form part of diocesan structures.

Therefore, a diocesan Education Department should not automatically be treated as the same structure as the **Provincial Education** function.

---

## Education and Human Development

The ACK connects education with human development.

The Board of Education and Training describes education as a means of reducing ignorance for the advancement of the Church and mankind.

Education therefore contributes to wider objectives such as:

* Personal development.
* Leadership development.
* Christian service.
* Community development.
* Skills development.
* Social responsibility.
* Institutional development.

---

## Relationship with Other ACK Boards

Education interacts with several other areas of ACK ministry.

### Board of Mission

Education supports Christian formation, evangelism and ministry development.

### Board of Social Services

Education can contribute to community development, social transformation and addressing educational inequalities.

### Board of Finance

Educational institutions and programmes require appropriate financial planning, accountability and resource management.

The relationship between Boards should be recorded as functional collaboration rather than assuming that one Board directly controls another.

## Current Governance Context

The official ACK Board of Education page currently identifies **The Rt. Rev. Dr. Christopher Rutto**, Bishop of the Diocese of Eldoret, as Chairperson of the Education and Training Board.

It also identifies committees dealing with areas including Education, Liturgy, and TEE & Chaplaincy.

Current office-holder information should always be rechecked against the official ACK source before being used as a permanent knowledge-base fact because leadership positions can change.

## Data Quality Notes

For the ACK knowledge base:

* Distinguish the **provincial Education function** from diocesan Education Departments.
* Do not treat individual schools as provincial departments.
* Record educational institutions separately when detailed institutional information is required.
* Distinguish schools, colleges, universities and theological institutions.
* Record scholarship and student-support programmes separately when they have distinct structures.
* Distinguish education from theological education.
* Distinguish education from chaplaincy.
* Verify current leadership and institutional status from official ACK or institutional sources.
* Preserve historical education information separately from current organisational structures.
* Do not assume that an ACK-affiliated institution is directly administered by the Provincial Board.
