# Anglican Church of Kenya – Kenya Anglican Youth Organisation (KAYO)

## 1. Overview

The **Kenya Anglican Youth Organisation (KAYO)** is the youth ministry of the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK) and operates under the **Board of Mission**.

KAYO provides a structure through which young people participate in Christian fellowship, discipleship, leadership development, evangelism, education, personal development and service to the Church and society.

The organisation operates across the dioceses of the Anglican Church of Kenya.

---

## 2. History

The origins of KAYO can be traced to the establishment of the **Anglican Youth Organization (AYO)**.

In **1962**, the then Bishop of the former Mt. Kenya Diocese, the late **Bishop Obadiah Kariuki**, developed a vision for young people and established the Anglican Youth Organization.

The principal aim was to bring young people back into the mainstream of the Church of Christ.

In **1964**, the Bishop launched the Diocesan Anglican Youth Organization at **St. Paul’s Mother Church, Kabete** and commissioned a Youth Committee to undertake extension work within the Diocese.

During a subsequent Provincial Synod, the other Bishops agreed to establish AYO in their dioceses.

The name was subsequently changed from **AYO to KAYO – Kenya Anglican Youth Organisation** in order to provide a national structure covering the Anglican Church across Kenya.

---

## 3. Position within ACK

KAYO is one of the departments currently identified by the ACK under the **Board of Mission**.

The current Board of Mission identifies four departments:

1. Mothers’ Union (MU)
2. Kenya Anglican Men’s Association (KAMA)
3. Kenya Anglican Youth Organisation (KAYO)
4. Children’s Mission

The Board of Mission focuses on spreading the Gospel, evangelism, discipleship, preaching and helping Christians develop a strong foundation in the Christian faith.

---

## 4. KAYO Mandate

The ACK's official history of KAYO records several principles that shaped its original youth mandate.

These include:

### 4.1 Respect for Young People

Young people are to be recognised and respected as persons.

### 4.2 Positive Christian Identity

KAYO seeks to help young people develop a positive self-image rooted in Jesus Christ.

### 4.3 Acceptance

Young people are to be accepted as they are while being guided in their Christian growth.

### 4.4 Close Relationships

KAYO emphasises developing close and meaningful relationships with young people.

The original teaching encouraged leaders to work **with** young people rather than simply working **for** them.

### 4.5 Spiritual Leadership

KAYO seeks to provide spiritual guidance and authority to young people through active participation and accompaniment.

These principles form part of the historical foundation of the organisation's youth ministry.

---

## 5. Motto

The KAYO motto is:

> **“Be strong in the Lord.”**

The motto reflects the organisation's emphasis on strengthening young people in their Christian faith and service.

---

## 6. Membership

The ACK's official KAYO page states that membership comprises young people **above the age of 18 to 35 years**, with most members being unmarried.

KAYO therefore provides a structured youth ministry for young adult members of the Anglican Church.

**Data note:** Age and membership requirements should be checked against the latest KAYO constitution or official provincial guidance when more detailed membership rules are required.

## 7. Core Areas of KAYO Ministry

KAYO's ministry includes several major areas.

### 7.1 Christian Formation

KAYO supports young people in developing their Christian faith through:

* Bible teaching
* Prayer
* Fellowship
* Worship
* Discipleship
* Christian mentorship

The aim is to help young people develop a strong and active Christian identity.

### 7.2 Leadership Development

KAYO provides opportunities for young people to develop leadership skills.

Activities may include:

* Leadership training
* Conferences
* Youth committees
* Mentorship
* Service opportunities
* Participation in Church leadership structures

The organisation has historically contributed to the development of lay and Church leaders.

### 7.3 Personal Development

KAYO encourages holistic development of young people.

This includes:

* Personal growth
* Education
* Life skills
* Entrepreneurship
* Career development
* Responsible citizenship

### 7.4 Evangelism and Mission

As part of the Board of Mission, KAYO contributes to the wider mission of ACK.

Young people participate in:

* Evangelism
* Christian outreach
* Youth services
* Mission activities
* Community engagement

### 7.5 Economic Empowerment

KAYO members have initiated income-generating projects and received entrepreneurial training.

The ACK gives examples including:

* Shoemaking
* Production and sale of tablecloths
* Construction of rental houses

These activities demonstrate the organisation's emphasis on practical economic empowerment and employment opportunities.

### 7.6 Education

KAYO encourages young people to pursue education and develop skills that contribute to their personal and professional growth.

---

## 8. Conferences and Gatherings

KAYO leadership organises conferences at both **diocesan and provincial levels**.

These gatherings provide opportunities for young people to:

* Learn about the Christian faith
* Develop personally
* Build relationships
* Receive mentorship
* Strengthen their leadership abilities
* Participate in fellowship

Conferences are therefore an important part of KAYO's youth ministry structure.

# 9. Anglican Students’ Fellowship of Kenya (ASF-K)

The **Anglican Students’ Fellowship of Kenya (ASF-K)** is a ministry within KAYO serving Anglican youth in institutions of higher learning.

ASF-K addresses issues affecting Anglican students and seeks to contribute to their holistic growth as Christians within the Anglican tradition.

Its mission is to nurture Anglican students and help them become agents of change on campus, within the Church and in their surrounding communities.

---

## 10. ASF-K Structure

According to ACK, ASF-K is headed by the **KAYO Provincial Executive** together with the **ASF Provincial Committee**.

The ASF Provincial Committee includes:

* Chairperson
* Anglican Students’ Fellowship Organiser (ASFO)
* Events and Media Coordinator
* Treasurer
* Secretary
* Two Chapter Leaders

Other structures include:

### Regional Chapter Committees

These bring together dioceses within a region.

### Diocesan Chapter Committees

These coordinate institutions within a particular diocese.

### Institutional Committees

These operate within individual institutions of higher learning.

This provides ASF-K with a structure connecting students, dioceses and the wider KAYO ministry.

---

## 15. Diocesan Presence

KAYO has been established across the dioceses of the Anglican Church of Kenya.

The ACK states that most dioceses have a **full-time youth worker** who assists youth committees with planning their activities.

The diocesan structures allow KAYO activities to respond to the particular needs and circumstances of young people within each diocese.

**Data note:** Diocesan youth workers, chairpersons, secretaries and other office holders should be recorded at diocesan level and verified against current diocesan information.

---

## 16. Relationship with the Board of Mission

KAYO operates under the **Board of Mission**.

The Board of Mission coordinates ACK's mission work with the dioceses through respective departments.

KAYO contributes to this mission through:

* Youth evangelism
* Christian formation
* Discipleship
* Leadership development
* Youth fellowship
* Mentorship
* Community service
* Personal development

---

## 17. KAYO and Youth Leadership

KAYO provides young people with opportunities to participate actively in the life of the Church.

Through youth committees, conferences, fellowship, ministry and service, young people can develop leadership experience and contribute to Church programmes.

The ACK notes that KAYO has produced lay leaders, Church leaders at different levels and committed Church members.
