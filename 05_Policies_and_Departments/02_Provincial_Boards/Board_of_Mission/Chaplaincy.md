# Anglican Church of Kenya – Chaplaincy

## 1. Overview

**Chaplaincy** is a ministry of the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK) that provides Christian pastoral care, spiritual guidance, worship and ministry within institutions and specialised environments.

Chaplaincy forms part of the wider mission work of ACK and is identified within the Church's Board of Mission structures.

ACK chaplaincy serves people in settings where ordinary parish-based ministry may not always be readily accessible, including:

* Learning institutions
* Hospitals and health facilities
* Prisons
* Airports
* Ports
* Disciplined and uniformed services
* Other institutions and specialised settings

ACK describes chaplaincy as part of its broader mission of bringing Christian ministry to people in their particular places of work, study, service or care.

## 3. Purpose of Chaplaincy

The purpose of ACK chaplaincy is to extend Christian pastoral ministry into institutional and specialised environments.

Its work may include:

* Pastoral care
* Prayer
* Christian counselling and spiritual support
* Worship services
* Bible teaching
* Evangelism
* Spiritual encouragement
* Support during difficult circumstances
* Ministry to individuals and communities within institutions

Chaplaincy enables the Church to respond to people within their particular institutional contexts.

---

## 4. Major Chaplaincy Areas

### 4.1 Learning Institutions

ACK provides chaplaincy ministry within educational environments.

These may include:

* Schools
* Colleges
* Universities
* Other learning institutions

The ministry supports students, teachers, staff and institutional communities through Christian teaching, worship, pastoral care and spiritual guidance.

ACK's education and training work also recognises chaplaincy as an important means of providing Christian models of service within educational institutions.

---

## 5. Hospital Chaplaincy

Hospital chaplaincy provides pastoral and spiritual ministry within healthcare environments.

A hospital chaplain may minister to:

* Patients
* Families
* Healthcare workers
* Other people within the hospital community

Activities may include:

* Prayer
* Pastoral visitation
* Spiritual encouragement
* Worship
* Christian counselling and support

Hospital chaplaincy allows the Church to offer spiritual care to people experiencing illness, treatment, recovery or difficult circumstances.

---

## 6. Prison Chaplaincy

ACK also provides chaplaincy ministry within prisons.

Prison chaplaincy provides opportunities for:

* Worship
* Prayer
* Bible teaching
* Pastoral ministry
* Spiritual counselling
* Christian rehabilitation and transformation
* Support for people within correctional institutions

The ministry seeks to make Christian pastoral care available within the prison environment.

---

## 7. Chaplaincy in the Disciplined and Uniformed Forces

Chaplaincy has a significant role within Kenya's disciplined and uniformed services.

ACK's official publications identify chaplaincy among the areas in which the Church provides ministry, with particularly strong involvement in the disciplined and uniformed forces.

ACK also states that the Archbishop serves as the **Bishop in Ordinary to the Kenya Defence Forces**.

Chaplaincy within the forces can include:

* Worship
* Prayer
* Pastoral care
* Spiritual guidance
* Christian counselling
* Ministry to personnel and their families

---

## 8. Airport and Port Chaplaincy

ACK's official publications also identify chaplaincy in:

* Airports
* Ports

These specialised settings bring together people from different backgrounds and locations.

Chaplaincy provides a way for the Church to offer pastoral and spiritual ministry within these environments.

## 9. Core Functions

For knowledge-base classification, ACK chaplaincy can be understood through the following core functions.

### 9.1 Pastoral Care

Providing spiritual and pastoral support to people within institutions.

### 9.2 Worship

Facilitating Christian worship in institutional environments.

### 9.3 Prayer

Providing opportunities for prayer and spiritual encouragement.

### 9.4 Christian Teaching

Supporting Christian formation through:

* Bible teaching
* Christian education
* Discipleship
* Spiritual formation

### 9.5 Evangelism

Sharing the Christian faith and supporting the wider mission of the Church.

### 9.6 Counselling and Spiritual Support

Providing appropriate spiritual and pastoral support to people facing difficult circumstances.

### 9.7 Institutional Ministry

Working within the policies and requirements of the institution while maintaining the Church's pastoral and spiritual mandate.

## 10. Chaplaincy and the Wider Mission of ACK

Chaplaincy forms part of ACK's wider mission ministry.

The Church's official mission information identifies children's ministry, youth ministry, Mothers' Union, KAMA, chaplaincy, pastoral ministry and worship among its mission activities.

Chaplaincy therefore complements parish and diocesan ministry by taking Christian pastoral care into specialised environments.

---

## 11. Training of Chaplains

ACK recognises the importance of training clergy and other ministry personnel for effective chaplaincy.

The Church's strategic approach includes theological and ministry training institutions and programmes that contribute to the preparation of chaplains.

ACK's official education information states that the Church seeks to train chaplains for Christian ministry in areas including:

* Schools
* Hospitals
* Institutions of higher learning
* Disciplined forces

Training helps chaplains understand both Christian ministry and the particular requirements of the environments in which they serve.

---

## 12. Chaplaincy and Professional Environments

Chaplaincy requires ministry that is sensitive to the environment in which it operates.

Depending on the institution, chaplains may need to work within:

* Institutional regulations
* Professional standards
* Confidentiality requirements
* Security procedures
* Safeguarding requirements
* Existing pastoral and administrative structures

The exact responsibilities of a chaplain therefore depend on the institution and the relevant ACK or institutional policies.

---

## 13. Provincial and Diocesan Relationship

Chaplaincy operates within the wider structures of the Anglican Church of Kenya.

Provincial-level coordination provides a broader framework, while dioceses and local ministry structures may support chaplaincy work within their geographical areas.

ACK diocesan information demonstrates that chaplaincy can appear alongside other diocesan ministry departments, including:

* Hospital Chaplaincy
* Prisons' Chaplaincy
* Missionary Evangelism
* KAYO
* KAMA
* Mothers' Union
* Education

The exact departmental arrangement may differ between dioceses.

---

## 14. Chaplaincy and the Board of Education and Training

Chaplaincy also has an important connection with ACK's education and training structures.

ACK's education information states that chaplaincy is part of the Church's engagement with educational institutions and other sectors.

The ACK Decade Strategy 2018–2027 also identifies a **TEE & Chaplaincy Committee** under the Board of Education & Training.

Therefore, the ACK Hub should not assume that every reference to chaplaincy belongs exclusively to one administrative department.

Instead, records should distinguish:

* Board of Mission chaplaincy
* Education and training-related chaplaincy
* Diocesan chaplaincy
* Institution-specific chaplaincy

---

## 15. Historical and Current Structure

ACK documents from different periods use somewhat different descriptions of the Church's organisational structure.

For example, the ACK Decade Strategy 2018–2027 lists a **Chaplaincy Committee** under the Board of Mission, while the current ACK website identifies chaplaincy within the Board of Mission and lists a current Chaplaincy Secretary.

The ACK Hub should therefore preserve the date and source of organisational information instead of automatically treating every historical committee structure as the current structure.

2. **Historical information** – earlier chaplaincy committees and organisational arrangements.
3. **Institutional information** – chaplaincy in a specific institution.
4. **Diocesan information** – chaplaincy structures belonging to a particular diocese.
5. **Training information** – programmes and institutions involved in chaplain preparation.

Current office holders and institutional assignments should always be verified against the latest official ACK or relevant institutional source.
