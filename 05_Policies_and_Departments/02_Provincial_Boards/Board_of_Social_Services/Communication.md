# ACK Communication

## 1. Overview

**Communication** is one of the major areas of concern under the **Board of Social Services** of the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

The communication function supports dialogue between the Church, its members and the wider society.

The ACK describes communication as part of the Board's role in addressing major areas of societal concern alongside:

* Development
* Justice
* Peace and reconciliation
* Research into societal problems and positive solutions

## 2. Purpose

ACK communication serves to:

* Share information about the Church and its ministry
* Communicate the Church's position and activities to members and the wider public
* Support dialogue between the Church and society
* Share information about social and community concerns
* Document and communicate development and transformation work
* Provide platforms for reflection on issues affecting society
* Strengthen awareness of the Church's mission and activities

## 3. ACK Publications

The official ACK Board of Social Services page identifies two publications as important communication vehicles:

### 3.1 The Christian Voice

**The Christian Voice** is an ACK publication used as a communication and dialogue platform.

It provides a channel through which the Church can communicate with its members and engage wider societal issues.

### 3.2 Roots

**Roots** is another ACK publication identified by the Church as a vehicle for dialogue.

Together with The Christian Voice, it forms part of the communication approach associated with the Board of Social Services.

## 4. Communication and Social Transformation

Communication supports the wider social-service mandate of the ACK.

It can be used to:

* Raise awareness of social issues
* Share development experiences
* Promote justice and reconciliation
* Communicate community concerns
* Encourage responsible citizenship
* Share information about Church programmes
* Promote positive social change
* Facilitate dialogue between different groups

## 5. Relationship with Development Work

Communication is closely connected with development and social transformation.

Development programmes generate information, experiences and lessons that need to be communicated to:

* Church members
* Communities
* Dioceses
* Development partners
* Government and other institutions
* The wider public

Communication therefore supports visibility, knowledge sharing and learning within the ACK's social-development work.

## 6. Communication and the Wider ACK Structure

Communication should be understood as a **functional area of the Board of Social Services**, rather than automatically treating it as a separate Provincial Board.

The Board's wider areas of work include:

**Development → Communication → Justice → Peace & Reconciliation → Research and Social Transformation**

These areas are interconnected in addressing societal concerns.

## 7. Digital and Contemporary Communication

As the Church adopts modern communication technologies, ACK communication may also involve digital platforms and online information sharing.

For the ACK Hub, digital communication records should distinguish between:

* Official ACK announcements
* Official ACK publications
* Diocesan communications
* Programme communications
* Social-media content
* Historical publications

Only authoritative sources should be treated as official institutional information.

## 9. Data-Quality Guidance

Communication information can change frequently.

For current information, priority should be given to:

1. Official ACK website
2. Official ACK publications
3. Official provincial announcements
4. Official diocesan websites and publications
5. Other authorised ACK communication channels

Social-media posts and third-party reports should not automatically be treated as authoritative ACK records.
