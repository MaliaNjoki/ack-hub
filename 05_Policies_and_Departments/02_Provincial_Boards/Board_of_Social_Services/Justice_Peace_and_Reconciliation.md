# ACK Justice, Peace and Reconciliation

## 1. Overview

**Justice, Peace and Reconciliation** is a major area of concern under the **Board of Social Services** of the Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK).

The work forms part of the Church's wider social-development and transformation mandate and responds to social challenges affecting individuals, families and communities.

The ACK identifies justice, peace and reconciliation alongside development, communication and research as areas through which the Church engages with societal concerns.

## 2. Purpose

The ACK's work in justice, peace and reconciliation seeks to contribute to societies where:

* Human dignity is respected
* Communities can live peacefully together
* Conflicts can be addressed constructively
* Vulnerable people are protected
* Justice and fairness are promoted
* Communities participate in addressing issues that affect them
* Reconciliation can take place after conflict or division

## 3. Justice

Justice-related work may involve addressing issues affecting communities and promoting:

* Human rights
* Equality and fairness
* Good governance
* Accountability
* Protection of vulnerable groups
* Access to justice
* Respect for the rule of law
* Responsible citizenship

The Church may engage these issues through advocacy, public dialogue, community programmes and partnerships.

## 4. Peacebuilding

Peacebuilding focuses on preventing and addressing conflict and strengthening peaceful relationships within communities.

Relevant activities may include:

* Peace education
* Conflict prevention
* Dialogue
* Community reconciliation
* Mediation and peacebuilding initiatives
* National cohesion
* Community participation
* Early identification of social tensions

Peacebuilding may take place at provincial, diocesan and community levels depending on the programme and context.

## 5. Reconciliation

Reconciliation is concerned with rebuilding relationships where communities or individuals have experienced conflict, division or other forms of social disruption.

The approach can include:

* Dialogue
* Listening
* Community engagement
* Addressing causes of conflict
* Restoring relationships
* Promoting forgiveness and peaceful coexistence
* Supporting communities to move forward after conflict

Reconciliation should be understood as a process rather than a single event.

## 6. Advocacy

Justice, peace and reconciliation are closely connected to the ACK's advocacy work.

Advocacy may involve:

* Raising awareness of social issues
* Representing community concerns
* Engaging decision-makers
* Promoting human rights
* Supporting vulnerable communities
* Encouraging accountable leadership
* Promoting peaceful and inclusive communities

The Church may undertake advocacy directly or work with development and ecumenical partners.

## 7. Vulnerable Groups

The ACK's wider social-service mandate includes concern for people who experience vulnerability or disadvantage.

Justice and peace work may therefore include attention to:

* Children
* Young people
* Women
* Persons with disabilities
* Marginalised communities
* People affected by poverty
* Communities affected by conflict or displacement

Specific programmes and target groups should be documented separately where an official ACK source identifies them.

## 8. Relationship with Development

Justice, peace and reconciliation are interconnected with development.

Social and economic challenges can contribute to conflict and vulnerability, while conflict can undermine development.

The ACK therefore approaches social transformation through interconnected areas including:

**Development → Justice → Peacebuilding → Reconciliation → Social Transformation**

This work can be supported by ADS Kenya, diocesan development structures, Church programmes and partnerships.

## 9. Provincial and Diocesan Implementation

The Provincial Board provides the broader framework for the ACK's social-services mandate.

Implementation may take place through:

* Provincial structures
* Diocesan structures
* Community programmes
* Development organisations
* Partnerships and networks

The exact structure of a particular justice, peace or reconciliation programme should be verified from the relevant official ACK source.

## 10. Historical and Current Structures

The ACK has implemented different peace, justice and development initiatives at different points in its history.

For the ACK Hub, historical programmes, committees and projects should not automatically be presented as current provincial departments.

Where a source describes a structure from a particular period, the relevant date or period should be recorded.
