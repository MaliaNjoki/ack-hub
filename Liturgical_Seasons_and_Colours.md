# Liturgical Seasons and Colours (ACK)

## 1. Purpose

This file provides a quick-reference guide to the principal liturgical seasons and commonly associated liturgical colours for use within the ACK Hub.

It is intended to help the AI understand the relationship between liturgical seasons, colours and worship.

This file is a **reference aid** and does not replace the official Anglican Church of Kenya (ACK) Church Diary and Lectionary.

---

## 2. Authoritative Reference

The **current official ACK Church Diary and Lectionary** should be treated as the primary authority when determining the liturgical season, colour or observance for a particular date or service.

The ACK Decade Strategy 2018–2027 identifies the Church Diary and Lectionary as an ACK reference source.

Where this file differs from the current official Church Diary and Lectionary, the official ACK source takes precedence.

---

## 4. Advent

**Colour:** Purple / Violet

Advent is the season of preparation and expectation leading towards the celebration of Christmas.

The colour traditionally associated with Advent is purple or violet.

The season has both a forward-looking emphasis on the coming of Christ and a reflective character.

---

## 5. Christmas

**Colour:** White / Gold

White or gold is traditionally associated with Christmas.

The season celebrates the birth and incarnation of Jesus Christ and is characterised by joy and celebration.

---

## 6. Epiphany

**Colour:** White, followed by Green

White is associated with the Feast of the Epiphany and its celebration of the manifestation of Christ.

After the principal Epiphany observance, the liturgical colour may move into green as the Church continues through the period of Christian growth and teaching.

The exact sequence for particular dates should be checked against the current ACK Church Diary and Lectionary.

---

## 7. Lent

**Colour:** Purple / Violet

Lent is a period of preparation for Easter.

Purple or violet is traditionally associated with penitence, reflection and spiritual preparation.

Particular liturgical days within Lent may have their own observances or colour requirements.

---

## 8. Holy Week

**Colour:** Varies according to the day and service

Holy Week is the period leading from Palm Sunday through the events immediately preceding Easter.

The colour should **not be treated as one single colour for the entire week**.

For a particular Holy Week date or service, the AI should consult the current ACK Church Diary and Lectionary rather than infer the colour from a general seasonal rule.

This is particularly important for:

* Palm Sunday
* Maundy Thursday
* Good Friday
* Holy Saturday / Easter Vigil

---

## 9. Easter

**Colour:** White / Gold

White or gold is traditionally associated with Easter.

The Easter season celebrates the resurrection of Jesus Christ and is characterised by joy and celebration.

The Easter season extends beyond Easter Day, with the precise observances determined by the Church's liturgical calendar.

---

## 10. Pentecost

**Colour:** Red

Red is traditionally associated with Pentecost.

It represents the Holy Spirit and recalls the imagery of fire associated with the coming of the Holy Spirit upon the disciples.

---

## 11. Trinity Sunday

**Colour:** White

White is commonly associated with Trinity Sunday.

The day celebrates the Christian doctrine of the Holy Trinity: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

---

## 12. Ordinary Time / Trinity Season

**Colour:** Green

Green is traditionally associated with the periods of the liturgical year focused on continuing Christian life, discipleship and spiritual growth.

The terminology and precise structure of this period should follow the current ACK Church Diary and Lectionary.

---

## 13. Liturgical Colours as a Guide

Liturgical colours provide a visual expression of the character of a particular season or celebration.

They may be associated with themes such as:

* **Purple / Violet** — preparation, penitence and reflection
* **White / Gold** — celebration, holiness, joy and major celebrations of Christ
* **Red** — the Holy Spirit, fire, martyrdom and certain major celebrations
* **Green** — growth, discipleship and continuing Christian life

These descriptions provide general liturgical context and should not be treated as a substitute for the official ACK liturgical calendar.

---

## 14. Special Days and Services

The colour for a particular feast, saint's day, special service or other liturgical observance should not automatically be inferred from the general seasonal colour.

The AI should check the current official ACK Church Diary and Lectionary whenever a question concerns:

* A specific calendar date
* A particular feast
* A saint's day
* Holy Week
* Easter-related services
* Ordination services
* Special diocesan or provincial services
* Other exceptional liturgical celebrations

---

## 15. Local Practice

Local practice may vary in some circumstances.

Where an authorised diocesan or provincial ACK source provides a specific instruction for a service, the AI should identify the relevant authority and avoid presenting a local practice as a universal ACK rule.

---

## 16. AI Retrieval Rules

When answering questions about liturgical seasons and colours:

1. Identify the relevant liturgical season or date.
2. Check the current ACK Church Diary and Lectionary where available.
3. Do not infer a specific day's colour solely from the general seasonal colour.
4. Identify the source when a colour is disputed or uncertain.
5. Distinguish general Anglican practice from specifically verified ACK practice.
6. Treat current liturgical calendars as time-sensitive.
7. Do not invent colours for dates or services for which an authoritative source has not been verified.
